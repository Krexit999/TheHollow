## 0.2 THE LAWS

**LAW 1 — TWO KEYS.** Descent is blocked by exactly two things: **PRICE** (`dustCost(d) =
25·1.09^d`) and **HARDNESS**. Every system sits on one of those or on something that opens them.

**LAW 2 — STEPS, NOT STACKS.** Depth from transformations, never quantities. No recipe asks for
more than 8 of anything.

**LAW 3 — HIDE THE RECIPES, SHOW THE DESTINATIONS.** Forbidden: recipe browsers, requirement
lists, "build X to unlock Y", crafting trees, **and any list of unearned things with names on
them.** Required: a visible ladder of named *places*. *Show the mountain, hide the map.*

**LAW 4 — A MACHINE MUST CHANGE A VERB.** An unlock that delivers a number instead of a decision
is not an unlock.

**LAW 5 — EVERY REWARD OPENS A PROBLEM,** stated as a quoted player sentence or it isn't real.

**LAW 6 — SYSTEMS LAYER, THEY NEVER REPLACE.** Every entry names the earlier system it deepens.

**LAW 7 — NOTHING IS STATIC.** Every major system changes *in kind* four or five times.

**LAW 8 — BOTH SIDES OF A CHOICE MUST BE CAPABILITIES, AND THEY MUST MATTER IN DIFFERENT
PLACES *(new — the fix for eleven fake choices).*** A choice between a capability and a number
has one answer forever, because capability always wins. A choice between two capabilities that
are strong in *different shells, stations, tool states or objectives* changes its answer with
context instead of with knowledge. **The specification for every choice in this game is the
four that already worked** — shop forks, crush profiles, chip options, repair — and their shared
property is that both options are correct depending on a state that changes several times a
session.

**LAW 9 — A GATE MUST HAVE SKILL EXPRESSION.** No gate may be opened by repetition alone. "Do
this 200 times" is a toll, not a curve. Every gate is *do it once under a condition*, *do it to
a quality bar*, or *do it as part of something else.*

**LAW 10 — THE GAME MUST SURPRISE THE PLAYER *(new in v6).*** Progression may not consist solely
of unlocking expected upgrades. Every shell must contain discoveries, rare events, hidden
interactions, evolutions and long-term goals the player was never told existed. The game rewards
curiosity as much as efficiency. **The best moments are "I didn't know that could happen," not
"I unlocked +15%."**

> **Corollary, and it is a hard requirement:** every shell must contain at least one mechanic the
> player can **ACCIDENTALLY DISCOVER** — not unlock. Seven of them, one per shell, **none in the
> Codex count, none hinted on the Roll, none reachable by brute force.** They are listed in §47
> and they are the only things in this document that a player could finish the game without ever
> meeting.

## 0.4 THE FOUR PURSUITS, AND WHO THIS GAME IS FOR

| Pursuit | Chasing | Pays in |
|---|---|---|
| **THE DESCENT** | the next named station | depth, Cores, shells |
| **THE SEATS** | the terminal craft, one part per Recursion | permanence |
| **THE UNDERSTANDING** | propositions — rules you now know | capability inside systems you own |
| **THE PLANT** | the factory as an object of care | throughput, automation, headroom |

### The audience decision, stated as a decision

v4 filed this as a risk bullet. It is not a risk, it is a choice, and it should be made in the
open:

> **Loam is idle-viable. From Ferrite onward, this game requires hands.**

The Coil is Ferrite's only power and it is banked by chaining polarity by hand. The Prism
requires allocation. Cinder requires riding a gauge. An idle-leaning player will progress
normally through the first shell and **stop hard at the second**, and no amount of tuning fixes
that without gutting what makes those shells shells.

That breaches PILLARS.md pillar 1's locked ~5× idle bound. **Amend the pillar knowingly.** The
alternative — making every signature act automatable — deletes the reason each shell feels
different, which is the design's central claim.

What this buys the idle player instead: **Loam is a complete, satisfying, ~5-hour game** with a
floor, a Warden, a Seat and an ending beat. That is an honest offer. Pretending the other six
shells are available to them is not.

### 0.5.3 The cadence rule, re-audited as BEAT DENSITY

v4's rule was "a new verb every 3–10 hours," audited against a clock that did not exist. The
rule it was *trying* to protect is this, and it is auditable:

> **Every shell must open at least six new verbs, and no third of a shell may open zero.**

| Shell | Verbs opened | Thin third? |
|---|---|---|
| Loam | 19 | no |
| Ferrite | 6 | no |
| Verdance | 6 | no |
| Glassmere | 6 | no |

**Two honest failures.** Hollow opens three verbs against a floor of six, and Aleph two. Both

is to shorten it, not to add a fourth verb to it.

**Aleph is not fixed and I am not going to pretend otherwise:** it is two verbs and a boss. It
works because it is the shortest shell and the Seating is on the other side.

# 1. THE ROLL — the ladder of named places

**The shaft is a numbered, named list of STATIONS.** 109 across seven shells. Between them the
rock is procedural. Panel: one row per station at 380px.

```
▸ 44  BRICKLIGHT          hardness 2      —              WALL
  47  THE LONG CUT        hardness 2      graveclay      WRECK
  60  SINTER ROW          hardness 2      loamiron       WRECK
  72  The Ashfall         ·               ·              ·
 150  DEEPGRAVE                                          FLOOR
```

**Visibility:** the next three are fully legible (name, depth, hardness, seam, type). Everything
below shows **name and depth only**. The floor is always pinned at the bottom from the moment
you enter the shell. Prospecting (§9.4) burns fog off one distant station at a time.

**Types:** SEAM · WALL · WRECK · CHAMBER · HAZARD · LAIR · DIG · REST · FLOOR.

**Clearance persists.** Breaking a WALL is a permanent record through Collapse, Breach and
Recursion. You keep the road; you lose the tools.

## 1.1 THE RE-ROLL — *the fix for "consumed in one pass"*

The stress test's §1.2 finding is the most serious structural criticism v4 received: measured
cadence is 7–11 Collapses per Loam arc against 15 Loam stations, so a player walks the same
named rows forty-plus times and the document's centrepiece becomes scenery by hour 20.

**The fix: a station keeps its name, depth, type and hardness. Its CONTENTS re-roll within a
band on every Collapse.**

| Field | Persists | Re-rolls |
|---|---|---|
| Name, depth, type | ✔ forever | — |
| Hardness requirement | ✔ (and clearance with it) | — |
| **Seam** — which materials | — | ✔ within the shell's band-appropriate set |
| **Feature** — geode / journal / dry / Sign | — | ✔ |
| **Hazard intensity** | — | ✔ within a range |
| **A WRECK's contents** | ✔ once looted, it is yours forever | it becomes a **WORKS** — see below |

So *The Ashfall* is always The Ashfall: hazard, dust, at depth 72. What it holds this time is
not what it held last time. **The place is authored; the contents are generated.** 109 rows buy
a permanent geography instead of a consumable list, and the Assay Call (§40) has something to be
uncertain about on the fortieth visit.

**A looted WRECK becomes a WORKS**, a station where a machine you own was found. After a
Recursion, WORKS stations carry **your own previous plant's wreckage** — which is where
Archaeology's blueprints-with-your-name come from (§30) and why the Roll gets *more* personal
across a playthrough rather than less.

**LAW 5:** *"I shored the Ashfall to keep the drift and now its seam is frozen on the one roll I
didn't want."* Shoring (§9.4) trades the re-roll for the fast descent, which makes it a real
decision instead of a pure upgrade.

# 2. THE FACE — COMPACTION AND **GRAIN**

**Thirty percent of playtime passes through this one panel and in v4 it ran on a single
counter.** The stress test called it the highest-leverage unbuilt thing in the document. This is
its second axis, and it is deliberately the deepest section in v5.

## 2.1 What a cell is

Every cell carries **charge** (regenerating), **compaction** (0–26), and **GRAIN** — a direction,
one of four, drawn when the band generates and shown as a tick on the cell.

## 2.2 The two ways to hit a cell

| | **WITH the grain** | **ACROSS the grain** |
|---|---|---|
| Chip time | fast | ×1.8 |
| Dust | normal | ×1.3 |
| Compaction gained | +1 | **+3** |
| Side effect | none | **FRACTURE** |

**FRACTURE** is the whole system: an across-grain chip propagates **+1 compaction to the
adjacent cell along the grain line**, and that cell's own grain decides where it propagates
next. So a single well-aimed column of across-grain work sends a compaction wave through the
face in a direction you chose.

**This is how you aim compaction**, and compaction is the sole source of deep-entry materials
(§16.2) — the scarcest thing in the game. v4 made you grind one cell. v5 makes you *engineer a
seam*.

## 2.3 LOCK — the cost of working it wrong

A cell taken **across grain above compaction 20 LOCKS**: it becomes unworkable until the shell
re-rolls, and drills route around it. You lost a cell, permanently for this arc.

That is the first thing in this game you can lose by making a mistake with your hands, and it is
recoverable (a Collapse re-rolls the band) but expensive. **It also fixes the Patient Hand
doctrine's dead effect** — raising the lock threshold from 20 to 26 is now a real capability,
because it means you can drive a fracture wave four steps further before cells start dying.

## 2.4 Why this changes everything downstream

- **Drills gain a grain behaviour** (`with` = fast and shallow; `across` = slow and seeds
  compaction; `follow` = chase the fracture front). Drill loadouts stop being about *where* and
  start being about *how*. This is also what makes drills a combat actor (§27).
- **SKIM** is with-grain by nature. **HOLD** is across. The five chip verbs are now
  mechanically distinct rather than flavour.
- **Figures** (§12.3) become grain patterns — a *furrow* is a straight with-grain run, a *fault*
  is a deliberate fracture chain. Cutting one is now a legible skill rather than a lucky
  accident.
- **The Assay Call** (§40) becomes readable: grain direction hints at what the band was doing
  when it formed.
- **E1 (§7.4)** transmits traits along fracture lines.

**Combinatorial, not authored:** 26 compaction levels × 4 grain directions × propagation
geometry on a growing grid. **One field per cell and one propagation rule buys a space the
player is still finding positions in at hour 60.** That is the cheapest real depth in this
document.

**LAW 5:** *"I drove a fracture line straight into the corner and locked four cells I needed."*

**Holy-shit moment:** the first time a fracture wave crosses the face and drops deep-entry
material from a cell you never touched. Lands ~hour 1.

**Watchable:** the fracture propagates one cell per chip with a visible tick, so a good run is a
line of numbers climbing across the grid in a direction you chose.

# 3. THE PLANT — **FLOW AND SURGE**

v4's Draw was one global scalar and a sort order, which the stress test correctly called *"the
entire inter-machine relationship in 41 machines."* Every plant was the same plant at a different
size.

## 3.1 Two numbers, not one

| | **FLOW** | **SURGE** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | sustained draw per second | a bank that discharges in one burst, refills slowly |
| Good for | continuous processes | anything that fires once and hard |
| Wasted by | machines that idle between cycles | machines that run constantly |

**Machines have a demand profile.** The Kiln is pure Flow. The Crusher is Surge (it spikes per
batch). The Refinery wants both. **A LINE is a single enormous Surge draw** — six machines
firing as one action — which is why a Line on a Flow-only plant crawls and a Line on a Surge
plant is devastating.

## 3.2 Every power plant has a shape

| Shell | Plant | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Loam | **The Hearth** | pure Flow, small |
| Ferrite | **The Coil** | **pure Surge** — a chain banks a burst |
| Verdance | **The Bloom** | Flow, scales with cells you refuse to mine |
| Glassmere | **The Prism** | Flow, with a Surge ceiling set by unallocated bands |
| Cinder | **The Boiler** | Surge that grows with heat — the more dangerous, the bigger the burst |
| Hollow | **The Null** | Flow that grows as the Silence worsens |

**Because signatures carry down on Breach, your power profile is a build.** A player who kept
the Coil and the Boiler runs an enormous-burst, low-sustain plant that lives on Lines. A player
who kept the Bloom and the Null runs a continuous plant that never stops but never spikes.
**Two plants at identical total throughput now behave completely differently**, and neither is
better — they are good at different machines.

## 3.3 What this fixes

- **The Core tree's fake choice** (§40): it is now **Flow capacity vs Surge capacity**, two
  capabilities, and which you want depends on your current plant shape and the shell you are in.
- **The Coil stops being "the Ferrite power source" and becomes the thing that makes Lines
  fire.** Chaining by hand at the face directly buys the biggest action in the plant, which is
  the tightest active→industrial link in the design.
- **The Line's efficiency rating** (previously the only interesting inter-machine number) is now
  one of several.

**LAW 5:** *"My whole plant is Surge and I just Breached into Verdance, where the only power
grows slowly and never spikes."*

# 4. THE TERMINAL CRAFT — THE SEVEN SEATS

**The world is an unfinished tool. You finish it.** Each SEAT is a part cast from a shell's
terminal material at `aberrant` purity, found at that shell's FLOOR station.

| Seat | Part | Shell | Material | Sits at the end of |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | Core | Loam | **Deepgrave** | compaction 26 under a fracture wave · Refinery V |
| II | Head | Ferrite | **Poleiron** | a four-metal alloy · Coil saturation |
| III | Handle | Verdance | **Heartwood** | a strain carried across three Collapses |
| IV | Edge | Glassmere | **Truelight** | a six-band Lens at full intensity |
| V | Binding | Cinder | **Slagglass** | the Retort at 96% pressure, unvented |
| VI | Sockets | Hollow | **Nought** | a Witness that has fixed 1,000 units |
| VII | Grip | Aleph | **RECORD** | your own history — see below |

**STOLEN — the ATM Star.** One component per mod, each that mod's own endgame, so every branch
is visibly a tributary. *Emotion:* a sprawling tree that is nonetheless **aimed**.

**RECORD cannot be mined, refined, reacted or transmuted.** It is made from your own history —
depth records, cleared walls, Codex entries, marks, propositions, alloys. The last component of
the longest craft in the game is **having played.**

**First sight:** at Breach 1 one tool slot gains a SEAT frame with your Loam Core in it, and six
empty outlines appear — unnamed, unexplained. Nothing tells you what they want until you stand on
that shell's floor. **Seven outlines, zero recipes.**

**Why you cannot rush it:** `aberrant` needs Refinery V needs Draw capacity 6, and the capacity

# 7. CROSS-SYSTEM INTERACTIONS AND **EMERGENCE**

The stress test's §6 was blunt and correct: v4's "cross-system" matrix was **18 authored pairs —
a lookup table indexed by two systems instead of one** — and the only genuine emergence in the
document was the Circuit's loops. This section keeps the matrix (authored interaction is still
good content) and adds the three things that produce outcomes nobody wrote.

## 7.1 The authored pairs — kept, compressed

| A × B | What it does that neither does alone |
|---|---|
| **Sorting × Prospecting** | a filter naming a trait you own no material for makes the Roll flag stations whose seam could satisfy it |
| **Essences × Alloying** | infusing before pouring expands the space of valid ratios |
| **Shoring × Collapse** | Collapse becomes a re-roll you aim — at the cost of freezing what you shored (§1.1) |
| **Spectrum × Still** | a band allocated to separation splits two traits from one unit |
| **Balance × trap materials** | worth is chain-depth, so the worst materials to build with are the best to spend |
| **Grain × Prospecting** | a band's grain direction is readable ahead, so fracture routes can be planned before you arrive |
| **Coil × Line** | banked Surge fires a six-step Line in one action |
| **Pressure × Refinery** | above 88% heat the Refinery can skip a band at off-spec risk |
| **Witness × Balance** | a Witness can fix undecided stock into something you have never mined |
| **Fallow × Sorting** | a filter selects which trait a strain inherits |
| **Patterns × Breaker** | salvage returns a *design*, not just material |
| **Reading × everything** | ~30 propositions are literally "system A may now affect system B" |
| **Cuts × signature** | a Charged Cut banks Coil Surge outside Ferrite |
| **Grain × drills** | a drill set to `follow` chases the fracture front you started by hand |

## 7.2 E2 — **HAZARDS LEAK INTO THE PLANT** *(best value-to-cost)*

Shell hazards currently affect the run. **Now they affect machines.** One condition value per
machine, one rule per shell:

- **Cinder** — a plant run hot warps. A `brittle` liner cracks and shatters periodically; a
  `warm` frame runs measurably better the longer it bakes.
- **Verdance** — an idle machine is **overgrown** and comes back carrying a cultivar trait.
- **Hollow** — an unattended machine's output arrives **undecided**.
- **Ferrite** — a machine near a long chain **magnetises**: it pulls filtered stock one band wider
  than its filter specifies, which is sometimes exactly wrong.
- **Glassmere** — a machine in an unallocated band runs at half and **loses no purity**.

Nobody authors *"my Crusher spent forty hours in Cinder and now it crushes better and shatters
every twenty cycles."* The rules permit it; your own scheduling produces it.

**Cost: MEDIUM.** One field per machine, five rules. Effects are small and reversible by
re-casting the part, so the balance risk is bounded.

## 7.3 E3 — **THE CIRCUIT READS THE WORLD** *(lowest cost, build first)*

The Circuit (§25) reads machine state. Widen its vocabulary to **face and station state**:

```
WHEN seam = umberjade        → route to the Hold, not the Crusher
WHEN cell compaction > 14    → switch drills to `follow`
WHEN station type = hazard   → hold the Line, bank Surge
WHEN grain = across          → run the Press
```

Players then write plant behaviour that responds to a **procedurally generated world**, and the
interaction between an authored circuit and an unauthored band is unpredictable *to the player
who wrote it.* That is the definition being asked for.

**Cost: LOW–MEDIUM.** No new panel; the Circuit exists. It also retroactively makes crews
situational and is the cheapest way to turn automation into stories rather than logs.

## 7.4 E1 — **TRAITS TRANSMIT THROUGH PROCESSES** *(highest value, highest risk)*

Traits currently sit on materials and are *read* by machines (§11.2 — which the stress test
correctly called "one lookup performed 41 times"). **Make them transmit.** A machine imparts a
weak echo of its own part-traits to what it processes:

- a `brittle` liner leaves output slightly brittle
- a `warm` Kiln warms what it fires
- a `keen` Crusher sharpens

**A plant develops a house style nobody specified.** Two players with identical recipes and
different machine-part materials produce measurably different stock, and a plant assembled from
junk produces materials with contradictory traits that fail interestingly.

**The cap and decay rule this needs, specified:**
1. A material carries at most **4 traits**, hard.
2. A transmitted trait arrives at **quarter strength**.
3. A transmitted trait **decays one step per subsequent process** and is gone after four.
4. A trait already present at full strength is **not** re-added; transmission only fills empty
   slots or refreshes decay.

Without those four rules a six-step Line compounds to nonsense. With them, transmission is a
gentle flavouring you can aim, and this is the mechanism that turns §11.2 from a lookup into the
deepest system in the game **at no new UI cost.**

**Cost: HIGH — prototype first on a three-machine Loam chain (Crusher → Washer → Refinery)
before committing.** This is the one that could ship broken.

## 7.5 What is deliberately not here

Random mutation tables, procedural recipes, dice rolls. Those produce **variance**, which players
read as unfairness. The three above share one property: the outcome space is not enumerable in
advance, but **every individual outcome is explicable after the fact.** That is the line between
emergence and noise.

# 9. EXPLORATION

Four kinds, all hanging off the Roll.

## 9.1 CUTS — lateral grids with a broken rule

Some stations open a **CUT**: a 4×4–6×6 second grid running a modified ruleset. Your tool and
drills work in it normally.

**Flooded** (instant refill, cap 2) · **Dead** (no regen; everything `pure` or better; finite) ·
**Charged** (all cells polarised, in any shell) · **Grown** (all fallow) · **Hot** (triple
pressure) · **Hushed** (every drop undecided) · **Knotted** (hardness +2, drops +2 rarity) ·
**Thin** (3×3, one cell of each shell's physics).

A Cut is how a shell's *signature* reaches you before you have that shell. A Charged Cut in Loam
is the first time a player sees polarity, with no explanation, as a thing they found.

**Skip test: no** — three materials per shell drop only in Cuts, and one is on the tier ladder.

## 9.2 SIGNS — and the **depth axis** *(fix for the Explorer/Collector convergence)*

A **SIGN** is an authored strangeness attached to a station, presenting as one line of odd text.
v4's version was investigate-once. **v5 gives every Sign four levels**, and pursuing costs a
station-visit each:

```
THE SAG — a seam that will not stay quiet
  I    LISTEN (free)            → it hums at a frequency you don't recognise
  II   CHIP AT THE HUM          → graveclay, and the hum moves one cell
  III  FOLLOW IT ACROSS GRAIN   → it is following a fracture line you cut
  IV   WORK IT TO 26            → it locks, and something comes out of the lock
```

**This is the fix for convergence 2.** The Explorer takes level I and moves on — he wants *more
places*. The Collector exhausts all four — she wants *this place*. Same content, opposite
actions, and the two players now visibly play differently in the same hours where v4 had them
doing identical things.

**Signs pay in Curios, research notes, Cut openings, and materials with no other source — never
in Dust.** ~15 authored (down from 35; see §43), each re-rolling its station on Collapse but
keeping its identity, so you meet the same strangeness in different places and eventually learn
what it wants.

Several Signs are the **only** hint that a mechanic exists at all: the Standing Wave (two power
plants interfering) is introduced by a Ferrite Sign and explained nowhere.

## 9.3 PROSPECTING — the Assay Bench

Spend time and Surge to **sample**: returns a station's rock class, grain distribution, seam
materials with purity bands, and at bench tier III its **deep-entry prediction**.

Under LAW 3 the Bench has one job: it **burns fog off the greyed part of the Roll**, one station
at a time. The ladder's fog is a resource you spend Draw on, which makes an information system an
economy without charging admission to basic readouts.

**Features:** sample current → sample ahead → deep-entry prediction → **staking** → standing
samples that survive Collapse.

## 9.4 SHORING AND DRIFTS

Spend Brick and cast parts to **shore** a band; it becomes a **drift** you fall through
instantly, and it survives Collapse. Drifts chain. **This is the Collapse fast-forward** and
without it the reset loop re-walks ground you have walked.

**And now it costs something (§1.1):** a shored band no longer re-rolls its contents. You have
traded a permanent seam for a permanent shortcut, and the choice depends entirely on whether this
band's current roll is one you want to keep.

# 10. RESEARCH — **THE READING**

Found at Quillrest (Loam 98) — Sable's desk, still set out.

## 10.1 Notes, propositions, proofs

**NOTES are earned by novelty, never repetition.** First time you chain eight, distil a `warm`
essence, lock a cell deliberately, overclock past two steps, clear a wall with no drills.

**PROPOSITIONS are sentences that change a rule.** ~45, across eight disciplines. Not "+10%
refining" — *"Charged materials retain polarity through melting."*

**PROOFS are behavioural.** To open a proposition you demonstrate the thing it is about:

> **Proposition:** *"A trait is a shape, not a substance."*
> **Proof:** distil three different traits from three materials of one family.
> **Effect (hidden until proven):** the Still can distil from an **alloy**.

This is the modpack questbook with fetch-quests replaced by behaviour. A Proof cannot be
satisfied by delivering anything; it sends you back into a system you own with new intent.

## 10.2 **Propositions are OFF the floor gates** *(fix for the Optimizer hard-stop)*

v4 gated four shell floors on propositions. The stress test found the failure: **notes come only
from novelty and cannot be farmed, so the player whose method is repetition of a known-best line
is structurally least able to generate the game's most gating currency.** The Optimizer gets
hard-stuck and no amount of skill helps him.

**Fix: a proposition is a shortcut, never a key.**

| Floor | The gate | The proposition |
|---|---|---|
| Thornwall (Verdance) | the wall is fallow rock — clear it by *letting a quadrant go feral and harvesting it* | *"What is refused, grows"* makes it fast |
| The Dark Pane (Glassmere) | compacted and unlit — clear it by *allocating three bands to one cell* | *"Light is slow in dense rock"* makes it cheap |
| Flashpoint (Cinder) | needs a Retort throughput one Kiln cannot feed — clear it by *running two Kilns* | *"Heat and pressure are one measurement"* lets the Boiler drive one |
| The Refusal to Be (Hollow) | needs undecided stock held at volume — clear it by *hoarding and not looking* | *"Absence has a grain"* lets you compact it instead |

**Every floor now has a brute-force route and an elegant route.** The Optimizer grinds through;
the Reader walks through. Nobody is stuck, and the proposition is still worth having.

## 10.3 What propositions do

Twenty of the forty-five, by discipline:

**Metallurgy** — *"Purity is a memory of the last process"* (twice-refined stock keeps its band
through one melt) · *"Coherence is agreement, not sameness"* (a third trait mediates an opposed
pair) · *"Deep material is not rarer, it is older"* (compaction 26 drops carry a second trait)

**Mechanics** — *"Draw is a pressure, not a quantity"* (surplus Flow raises output *quality*) ·
***"A line is one machine"*** (a Line counts as one machine against capacity) · *"Wear is
information"* (a worn part reports which stat it carried)

**Magnetism** — *"Charged materials retain polarity through melting"* (+25% Coil Surge) · *"Two
poles make a field, three make a knot"* (unlocks the Standing Wave)

**Growth** — *"What is refused, grows"* (fallow in every shell) · *"Inheritance is not memory"*
(a strain can inherit from an *essence*)

**Refraction** — *"Light is slow in dense rock"* (bands apply at double weight to compacted
cells) · *"A band unspent is not a band wasted"* (unallocated intensity returns as Flow at 1.5×)

**Pressure** — *"Heat and pressure are one measurement at two scales"* (the Boiler can drive the
Kiln) · *"A vent is a decision, not a valve"* (partial venting)

**Absence** — *"Absence has a grain"* (undecided stock can be compacted) · *"To name is to
choose"* (a Witness may name a material the world does not contain)

**Chemistry** — *"A catalyst is a witness"* (catalysts stop being consumed on failure) ·
*"Residue is unfinished, not spent"* (residue re-distils once)

**Under LAW 3** a proposition shows **its question and nothing else** — `Why does umberjade
refuse graveclay?` — plus the Proof for the one you are working. You never see an effect before
you have it.

# 11. ENGINEERING

## 11.1 The Forge

Seven parts (Head · Core · Edge · Binding · Handle · Grip · Sockets). Purity → magnitude; traits
→ character; coherence and instability. Fifteen tiers (§18). Casts are themselves cast parts.
Sprue, grog, billets, plates, rods, wire. The affinity/opinion/heirloom layer already in the
codebase. **Emergent tool classes** — a build whose parts agree in a recognised way earns a
*name*, discovered, never listed.

**This is the one Tier-1 system already specified at Tier-1 depth. Leave it alone.**

## 11.2 Every machine is a Tinkers item

A machine's part materials change its **behaviour**: a `keen` Crusher liner grinds finer, a
`trueseated` Refinery frame loses less band on failure, a `warm` Kiln hearthstone runs hotter, a
`charged` Coil winding banks more Surge.

The stress test called this "one lookup performed 41 times," and it was right about v4.
**§7.4's trait transmission is what turns it into a system**: your machine parts now *flavour
everything they touch*, so choosing them is choosing what your whole plant produces.

## 11.3 THE DESIGN BOARD

Compose a tool or machine **before you have the materials**. Pick parts, pick hypothetical
materials from anything in the Codex, see projected stats as a **band, not a number** (an exact
projection turns the 14 build axes into a solved calculator).

Press COMMIT and the design becomes a **goal**: it sits at the top of the Hold, and **the Assay
Bench and Sorting filters both read it** — stations whose seams could satisfy it get flagged on
the Roll, and filters generate from it in one tap.

**It is the only system in the game where the player writes the objective.**

## 11.35 TOOL TEMPERAMENT — *the tool has opinions* · **INVENTED**

§50 gives machines an identity earned by diet. The tool — the object you hold for the entire
game — had marks and levels. **That is backwards, and this fixes it.**

**Temperament is earned by HABITS, not counters.** Not *"mined 5,000 cells"* but *"this tool
spent most of its life in Ferrite"* · *always run unstable* · *always repaired before it broke* ·
*always with the grain* · *always at high pressure*. The tool notices **how you live**, not how
much you did.

### It is not irreversible, and that is the point

**Reforge freely. Re-cast every part. Change tiers. The temperament stays.** It is not a lock on
the object; it is a memory of the hand. **Only the Legacy erases it** — which is what makes it
worth a slot in the inheritance pool (§21.5), competing with a machine, a proposition, a mark and
a Curio.

### It biases the Forge through INSTABILITY, never through numbers

A temperament adds **instability** when you seat a part whose traits it fights, and removes some
when you seat one it favours. No stat changes. **You can always force it — you simply live with
slips** (§11.4).

That is the whole mechanism: **it uses machinery that already exists and adds none.**

| Temperament | Earned by | Favours | Fights |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PATIENT** | most of its working life on cells above compaction 14 | `dense` · `tough` | `light` · `springy` |
| **PRESSURE-TOUCHED** | most of its life worked above 80% heat | `warm` | `rime` |
| **POLED** | most of its life inside unbroken chains | `charged` | `hollow` |
| **TRUE** | overwhelmingly with-grain work | `trueseated` · `keen` | `brittle` |
| **CROSSGRAIN** | overwhelmingly across-grain and fracture work | `brittle` · `keen` | `trueseated` |
| **UNSTEADY** | run unstable most of its life; slips frequent | **more instability before slips worsen** — it is used to it | clean, precise builds seat *worse* |
| **KEPT** | always repaired before breaking, never once broken | repairs cost less; parts seat clean | **salvage** — it resists being broken down |
| **GREEN** | most of its life on fallow and living stock | `springy` · live stock | `dense` |
| **QUIET** | most of its life in Hollow, or on undecided stock | **can seat a part cast from unresolved material** | high-purity parts, which have too much identity |

### Yes — a temperament can fight a Seat you have already set

**This is the sharpest consequence in the addendum.** A Seat is seated permanently (§4). If your
tool becomes `QUIET` after you have already set the Loam Core — `dense`, `trueseated`, the most
fixed thing you own — **the temperament fights a part you cannot re-cast.** You carry the
instability for the rest of the life.

There is exactly one remedy: challenge #10's free Seat re-pour (§20.2), **which now has a second
job it did not have before.**

### The cost is foregone opportunity

Becoming `PRESSURE-TOUCHED` is a long stretch of **not playing safe.** Becoming `KEPT` is never
once letting a tool break, which means repairing early and often when you would rather push.
Nothing is spent; something is *not done*, for a long time, and that is the only cost a habit can
have.

**LAW 5:** *"My tool went PATIENT somewhere in Loam and now every springy Grip I need for
Verdance seats at three instability."*

**Holy-shit moment:** the tool tells you what you are, unprompted, after you have already been
it for twenty beats.

**Twelve tests:** skip **yes — you can ignore it and it will still happen to you**, which is
correct for a system about habit · deletion breaks **the Legacy pool's fifth option, challenge
#10's second job, the Forge's part-selection tension after the first Seat, and the tool's only
link to §2's grain and §36's heat** · fun at zero bonuses **yes — being described accurately by
the object in your hand** · unique **nothing else in the document reads how you live rather than
what you did** · deepens **the Forge and §11.4's instability** · placed **wherever you have been.**

## 11.4 INSTABILITY IS **OVERPAYABLE** — and this is where slips come from

v4 made instability a hard gate: above the threshold a tool *would not seat*. The stress test
found the consequence — **the absurd build is the thing the Theorycrafter wants and the thing
the system explicitly forbids**, which is why he and the Optimizer converge on the same tool.

**Fix: above the binding threshold the tool seats anyway and becomes UNSTABLE.** It works. Each
use carries a small chance of a **SLIP** — the tool does something *else*, drawn from its own
traits:

| Trait | Its slip |
|---|---|
| `charged` | discharges into an adjacent cell — a free chip you did not aim |
| `brittle` | shatters the cell entirely: full charge instantly, compaction lost |
| `hollow` | mines nothing and returns the swing |
| `warm` | adds heat (in Cinder, that is either free power or a flood) |
| `keen` | cuts across grain regardless of your aim — fractures where you did not choose |
| `springy` | the swing repeats on a random neighbour |

**Slips are not damage. They are unpredictability**, and the deeper the instability the more
often they fire.

**This fixes three things at once:** the Theorycrafter gets a band of strong-and-unreliable
builds the Optimizer refuses (convergence 1 broken); §39's repair option *"keep using it cracked
because the damaged version rolled a trait you like"* now has a mechanism — **damage raises
instability, and instability is where slips live**, so a cracked tool is a *different* tool, not
a worse one; and slips interact with grain, heat and the Circuit without anyone authoring the
interaction.

**LAW 5:** *"My tool slipped and shattered the cell I'd spent two minutes compacting."*

**Cost note:** slip rate needs a hard ceiling (~15%) or the tool stops being a tool. Tune the
ceiling, not the effects.

them on the chain rather than beside it.

## 12.5 Secrets

**Four.** Not in any count, not hinted anywhere, found only by doing something nobody would think
to do. One example and only one: **seating a tool's own salvaged part back into itself** produces
a class that exists in no registry.

# 14. THE SYSTEMS THAT CARRY THE DENSITY

## 14.1 ESSENCE WORK — the Still and the Infuser · **STOLEN** (Thaumcraft; PoE Essences)

*Mechanic:* items decompose into properties; properties reapply deterministically.
*Emotion:* **the world is made of properties, not things** — and crafting becomes planable
rather than a slot machine.

**THE STILL:** one unit at `pure`+, choose **one** of its traits, receive a vial plus residue.
The other traits are lost. **THE INFUSER:** material + vial → the material gains that trait. Cap
4; each trait past the material's natural count adds instability (§11.4).

**Why it is the biggest keystone:** from tier VII the part requirements are *trait sets*, not
materials, and **no natural material carries them.** Two routes exist — alloy it or infuse it —
and both are keystones.

**What it does to the registry:** every one of ~158 materials becomes a source of properties. A
common ore with a rare trait is worth mining at hour 60. **This single machine is what makes 158
materials justifiable.**

## 14.2 ALLOYING — the Alloy Crucible · **STOLEN** (Tinkers/GregTech)

*Mechanic:* pour 2–4 metals in a ratio; valid ratios are a sparse set in a large space.
*Emotion:* **discovery by guessing, cheaply** — an invalid ratio gives grog (filler), not
nothing, so experimenting costs almost nothing.

**Mandatory because** tier V Bindings need a 3-trait material and Ferrite ore carries two.
~60 discoverable alloys, duplicate fusion, alloy-only traits (`poled`, `sympathetic`) that no
ore carries.

## 14.3 SORTING — the Sieve · **INVENTED**

A filter is a saved predicate (`trait = dense AND purity ≥ pure`). Assign to any machine's input;
filters chain, and rejects route onward.

**Not a punishment.** Machines with no filter **idle**, showing `no target`. Nothing is ever
silently destroyed. The hard gates are the abrasive route to `flawless` and the fact that from
tier VII part recipes name **traits**, and only a filter selects by trait.

**Its best feature:** a filter can target a trait you own no material for, and the Roll then
flags stations whose seam could satisfy it. **The plant tells you what to go mine.**

## 14.4 TRANSMUTATION — the Balance · **STOLEN** (ProjectE's EMC)

*Mechanic:* one hidden worth per material makes the registry liquid.
*Emotion:* ProjectE's feeling is "the economy is solved," and **that emotion is deliberately
damped here** — worth is strictly lossy (60%, improving to 35%) and generates nothing.

**Mandatory because** every tier needs material from the shell *above*, and once you Breach that
shell is gone until Recursion. **The Balance is the only route back.**

Every conversion writes to your **worth ledger**, and worth knowledge feeds the Assay Call —
so transmuting teaches you how to gamble on seams, which is how this system closes a loop
instead of being a sink.

## 14.5 CHAINING — the Line · **STOLEN** (GregTech multiblocks / Satisfactory manifolds)

*Mechanic:* several machines run as one recipe. *Emotion:* **you built a machine that thinks,
then watched it work.**

3 → 6 slots. A line has an **efficiency rating** (how well members' throughputs match) and is a
**single enormous Surge draw** (§3), which is why the Coil and the Line are the same decision
seen twice.

**Honest note:** its skip test is *ergonomic* — you could hand-run everything. It is the one
keystone justified by operability rather than by a gate.

## 14.7 DRIFTS · **INVENTED** — see §9.4.

## 15.3 Parallel routes and side-chains that turn out to be mandatory

**Parallel routes (C.7 — mandatory destination, optional road):**

| Destination | Road A | Road B | Road C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Concentrate | Crusher → Washer | Leach Vat (tailings) | Sump (reworked band) |
| 3-trait material | Alloy Crucible | Still + Infuser | Centrifuge split + infuse |
| Binding value | gems (Lapidary) | runes (Rune Bench) | relics |
| Abrasive | Sieve shards | Press swarf | Lapidary polish |
| Out-of-shell material | the Balance | Spiral parallel world | Recursion |

**Side-chains that look optional and are not:**

- **The Sump** looks like a way to squeeze dead bands. It is the only economical source of
  low-grade concentrate in bulk, which is what the **Balance** eats to make cross-shell
  material, which is mandatory from tier V. You will discover this two eras after you built
  the Crusher reject row for a different reason. *(22.4 — two reasons, short one first.)*
- **Char** looks like a fuel optimisation. Cinder's valves are cast from **char-bonded
  slag**, and valves are the only way to survive Cinder.
- **Frit** looks like a way to use Slag. It is the Glassmere solvent, so the whole of era IV
  runs on a byproduct you started making in era I.
- **Residue** from the Still looks like waste. It is the **Infuser's carrier** — you cannot
  infuse without it, so distilling is self-supplying and the player finds that out by having
  a pile.
- **Grog** looks like failure. It is the cheapest machine-frame material, so a era of failed
  alloy experiments literally builds your next factory.

## 15.4 Machine tiers, restated as capability

Every machine runs **I–III**. A tier is **built from cast parts of a given tier plus Draw
capacity**, never bought with currency.

| Tier | Capability | Later requirement that forces it |
|---|---|---|
| I | commons only | — |
| II | **retains the input's purity band** | `pure` unreachable otherwise → tier III |
| III | **emits byproducts at all** | shards → abrasive → `flawless` → tier VI |

**TRIMMED FROM FIVE TO THREE (A.99), and this is a correction, not a deferral.**
`MAX_MACHINE_TIER` has been **3** for the entire life of the project, and all
twenty-two machines written before A.97 carry a three-rung ladder because their
authors read the code. This table said five, so A.97 wrote six-row ladders for
the Axiom Engine and the Seating and **four of those rows described tiers no
player could reach** — and the same belief put `tierOf(...) < 5` into two Seat
conditions, which made **Seat I and Seat IV permanently unsatisfiable**.

The two cut rows were *"IV — two recipes in parallel"* and *"V — batch: one cycle
per stack"*. Neither is deferred: raising the cap means authoring two new
capability sentences for each of twenty-four machines, which is content nobody
has asked for, and the three that exist already carry §15.4's argument (a tier
buys a KIND of freedom, never a bigger number). `tier-ladders.test.ts` fails the
build if any machine describes a rung above the cap, or gates on one.

**Upgrade slots** (2–4 per machine, each a small cast part): *Coupler* (Draw efficiency) ·
*Regulator* (purity retention) · *Sieve Plate* (byproduct rate) · *Twin Chamber* (queue two)
· *Trait Filter* (Sorting, per-machine) · *Governor Head* (overclock band) · *Lining*
(reduces wear on the shell's aggressive medium — Cinder heat, Hollow silence).

**41 machines × 3 tiers × up to 4 slots**, every one of them made of cast parts, every one
of them therefore downstream of the Forge. (The old line read "40 machines × 5 tiers ≈ 200
construction events and ~600 slot decisions"; both factors were wrong and the products
were arithmetic on them.)

## 16.3 Trap materials — *high magnitude, one trait that ruins you*

Eight materials with excellent purity ceilings and a trait that wrecks coherence in the part
they look perfect for. They are not a gotcha: the Assay Lens shows the trait, and the answer
is the **Still** — distil the bad trait out and you have the best material in the shell.
**Trap materials are the Still's tutorial**, and they exist from era I so the lesson is
waiting when the machine arrives. *(C.1 applied to a concept.)*

Examples: `millstone` (superb Core magnitude, `brittle` — the one trait a Core must not
have) · `rimeiron` (best Ferrite Edge, `warm`, which fights every Cinder part you will pair
it with later) · `bluesteel` (highest raw Head numbers in Verdance, `hollow`, which drops
your durability pool through the floor).

## 16.4 Orphan rescue — the honest audit

A registry of 158 will have materials nothing wants. Three mechanisms exist so that none of
them is dead, and they are the reason those three machines are in the design:

1. **The Still** — any material with a trait is a trait source. This alone rescues ~80% of
   the long tail, because trait rarity and material rarity are not correlated.
2. **The Balance** — any material has a worth, so any material converts (lossily) into any
   material you want. This rescues everything the Still doesn't.
3. **The Reaction Bench** — the natural home for the remainder as reagents (see 8).

**Ruling for the build:** if a material has no seam, no reaction, no trait worth distilling
and no part it is best-in-class for, **give it a reaction-reagent job or delete it.** A
registry entry that never appears in a decision is content-shaped padding, and there is no
prize for 158 over 130.

# 17. THE REACTION BENCH

The only **lateral** converter — everything else moves material up a ladder, this moves it
sideways — which makes it the home for orphan materials and the engine of discovery.
**System: CHEMISTRY.** ~45 reactions.

**How it plays:** two inputs, one catalyst slot, REACT. No recipe list, ever. One heuristic the
game never states and every player works out in an hour:

> **Materials sharing a trait react. Opposed traits react violently (higher yield, needs a
> catalyst). Nothing in common gives grog.**

**STOLEN — Cultist Simulator.** *Mechanic:* no recipe book at all; discovery by combination.
*Emotion:* **the rules are inferable**, so a correct guess feels like understanding rather than
luck. **Catalysts are consumed only on failure**, so reasoning is nearly free and guessing pays.

**The families:**

- **SOLVENTS (6)** — Lye (Ash+Silt) · **Flux** (loamiron+Trace, cat: charstone) · **Sap**
  *grown, not made* · Frit-wash (Frit+weepstone) · Quenchwater (Slag+rimeiron, cat: Char) ·
  **Hush** *condensed, not made*. **Two of six are not reactions**, which forces Verdance and
  Hollow into their signature systems to run the most basic machine in the plant.
- **QUENCH MEDIA (5)** — Brine · Oil · Ash-bath · Rime · **Pyre-bath** (the only route to
  tier-XI temper).
- **ABRASIVE BINDERS (3)** — Coarse · Fine · **Keen**. **The abrasive grade is the purity
  ceiling.**
- **REAGENTS (6)** — Bloom-feed · Coil-wax · Die-black · Witness-salt · Line-oil · Seat-mortar.
- **ORPHAN RESCUE (~10)** — two low-value materials into one mid-value one. This family exists
  so no registry entry is dead: millstone+grog → mortarstone · rimeiron+silt → coldclay ·
  weepstone+ash → dimglass · bluesteel+residue → dullsteel.
- **TRAIT PRECURSORS (5)** — half the potency of a distilled vial, a tenth of the cost, two beats
  earlier. **The parallel route that stops the Still being a hard wall.**
- **SHELL EXOTICA (7)** — one per shell at its floor, each feeding its Seat.

**Unskippable:** four solvents, the abrasive binder, and witness-salt. **It blocks three
keystones.**

# 19. SHELL CHEMISTRY — **two authored, four skinned**

v4 specified ~250 recipes across 12 machines × 6 shells and claimed it "multiplies the perceived
size of the game by about six." The stress test found it produced **one** wow moment. The moment
is real — the Breach's dead Washer is the shell transition's whole payload — but 250 rows is not
what it costs.

**v5 authors two shells' chemistry properly and skins the rest.**

### Authored in full: FERRITE and HOLLOW

Chosen because they are the two shells whose chemistry is *conceptually* different rather than
cosmetically:

| Machine | Ferrite | Hollow |
|---|---|---|
| Kiln | Dust→**Ingot**, emits Scale | Void→**Null**, emits *nothing* — and that nothing is a resource |

## 20.1 Purchasable progression — **machine levels are cut**

| Track | Currency | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **Face shop** (Blade, Soil, Roots + ~2 rows/shell) | shell chip currency | ~17 rows × 40–120 levels, **each forking at every tier** (§40) |
| **Machine tiers I–V** | cast parts + Draw capacity | 41 × 5 |
| **Machine slots** | cast parts | up to 160 decisions |
| **Drill upgrades** | Brick + heads | 24 drills × head / behaviour / **grain mode** / wear |
| **Core tree** | Cores | 14 nodes × 10, per shell, **Flow vs Surge** (§40) |
| **Skill tree** | XP | 66 nodes, free respec |
| **Shell Mastery** | depth records | 7 × 50 |
| **Echo / Axiom / Spiral / Legacy shops** | prestige currencies | ~60 combined |
| **Tool + gear** | materials | 15 tiers × 7 parts + 5 gear slots |
| **Codex** | discovery | ~180 entries |

**CUT: machine levels (500–1,000 purchases).** They produced zero wow moments, failed LAW 7

**Design rule unchanged:** no upgrade raises the field ceiling. Everything is reach, speed,
behaviour or capability.

## 20.2 Challenges — ten rule inversions

**STOLEN — Trimps' challenges / Realm Grinder's factions.** *Mechanic:* a run with a modified
rule whose reward is a permanent **rule change**. *Emotion:* **the restriction becomes a
capability** — you beat a limitation and keep a piece of it.

**SHIPPED A.103, AND THE TABLE BELOW IS THE ONE THAT SHIPPED.** The ten are one per SEAL —
`laws.ts` has exactly ten and every guard site had its challenge's name written in the comment
above it for phases, waiting. Each starts WHERE YOU STAND and is won by carrying it N depths
below where you began; abandoning costs the attempt and takes nothing.

| # | Inversion | The rule it bends | The permanent grant (a capability) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **THE UNATTENDED** | no chipping, no techniques; regen ×0.6 | drills leave the cell your hand last worked alone, without the boots |
| 2 | **THE LONG FALL** | you may not Collapse | **drifts survive the Breach**, re-timbered onto the new shell's ladder |
| 3 | **THE THIN SEAM** | nothing drops at all | a geode opens where it is found |
| 4 | **THE HONEST STONE** | every material at zero purity | a drop names its purity as it lands |
| 5 | **ONE CELL** | the face will not widen | the face may be turned on its side (W and H trade places) |
| 6 | **THE EMPTY HAND** | bare hands, whatever you carry | salvage hands the cast parts back instead of scrap |
| 7 | **COLD IRON** | the Kiln will not light | the Kiln never needs to recover between overstokes |
| 8 | **THE UNLIT** | nothing accrues while away | crews keep walking while you are away |
| 9 | **THE HELD BREATH** | Governor off, heat ×2 | the choke works in any shell, not only Cinder |
| 10 | **SABLE'S WALK** | only the Face; depth capped at 60 | a room you have opened never closes again |

**FOUR OF v6's GRANTS WERE NUMBERS AND WERE RE-CUT (A.103).** "+25% Surge", "the lock threshold
rises 20 → 26", "two essences per unit" and "the Line gains a slot" are each a permanent
multiplier hidden behind a restriction, which is the shape pillar 2 exists to refuse — and this
table is the one place in the document that asked for four of them. **A grant is a capability,
never a number**, enforced by test: `challenges.test.ts` holds all ten and asserts `dpsMax`
identical at equal depth. The four cut challenges (One Trait, Fallow, Blind, Brittle) had no seal
to express themselves through and would each have needed a new one; the ten that ship are the ten
the registry could already speak.

**WHERE THEY OPEN: the Spiral,** because §21's locked ladder puts them there. That reads late and
is correct — a Spiral rebuilds the world from `initialState`, so the inversions are what make the
SECOND climb differently shaped rather than merely faster. Every grant is worth something on a
re-climb and nothing on the run that earned it.

## 20.3 Beat density, not a clock

See §0.5.3. The rule is *every shell opens at least six verbs and no third of a shell opens
zero*, and the two failures (Hollow, Aleph) are named there rather than hidden.

# 21. THE RESET LADDER

Five layers. Each states its **want**, its **fast-forward**, and **what makes it not a repeat.**

## 21.1 COLLAPSE — *wants depth; gates the size of the plant*

- **Lose:** face upgrades, shell currency, depth.
- **Keep:** tool, materials, **machines**, drifts, clearances, Codex.
- **Gain:** Cores → **Flow and Surge capacity** (the only source) and `Y`.

**Fast-forward: DRIFTS.** By Collapse 8 the first 60 depths take under a minute.
**Not a repeat: THE RE-ROLL (§1.1).** Every station's seam, feature and hazard intensity
regenerate. You walk the same geography into different contents, and the Assay Call has
something to be wrong about every single time.
**Its decision:** the Core tree affords ~half of tranche 1 per arc — Flow now, or Surge for the
Line you are about to build.

## 21.2 BREACH — *wants a floor; gates the next physics*

- **Lose:** Cores, Core tree, shell currency, **machine tiers** (drop to I).
- **Keep:** tool, depth records, clearances, Codex, drifts (at Shoring IV), the machine roster,
  and **one aspect of the shell's signature, permanently.**
- **Gain:** the next shell. Echoes. **A SEAT.**

**Fast-forward: THE PATTERN BOOK.** Every machine you have built rebuilds in one click at a
tenth of the cost. You re-*seat* your factory rather than re-earn it.
**Not a repeat: THE CHEMISTRY (§19).** It rebuilds instantly and then does not work.
**Its choice — rebuilt under LAW 8 (§40):** each signature has two aspects, and **they are strong
in different shells below**, so the answer depends on your route rather than on power.

## 21.3 RECURSION — *wants the Core; gates how large a plant can ever be*

- **Lose:** all shells, Echoes, the plant, the tool.
- **Keep:** XP, depth records, Codex, the Margin, **and the Seats.**
- **Gain:** Axioms. **A permanently higher Draw capacity ceiling.**

**Fast-forward: `firstWord`** — start with Kiln, Drill Bay and Forge standing.
**Not a repeat: THE OBJECTIVE.** Recursion 1 reached Aleph. Recursion 2 is about **Seats III–IV**
— Verdance and Glassmere chemistry, a different plant emphasis, a different tool. You never
re-run a Recursion; you run the one that gets the Seats you lack.
**And the Margin fires:** your run compiles into journal fragments the next world seeds at depth,
in Sable's voice. Sable is a previous you.

## 21.4 SPIRAL — *wants attention*

Parallel worlds, rule-inversion challenges, the Automation Grid. Worlds you are not standing in
run at 15%.
**Not a repeat:** it is not a descent. **STOLEN — Legend of Idleon's multi-character
allocation.** *Mechanic:* N instances, and the game is deciding where *you* are. *Emotion:*
**triage** — the pleasure of being the scarce resource.

## 21.5 THE LEGACY — *wants to know what survives you*

- **Trigger:** complete the Seating.
- **Resets:** everything. Axioms, Seats, the Record, plant, tool, clearances. **More than any
  layer before it.**
- **Keeps:** **THE INHERITANCE — five things you pick from one pool** (§40), where the pool is
  *what you actually used this life.*
- **Gains:** Legacy currency; your journals seed the next world.

**Not a repeat, three ways:** the inheritance **rewrites the opening** (a life that starts with
the Still standing inverts the whole Loam economy); **your own wreckage is on the Roll** as WORKS
stations carrying blueprints with your name; and **Sable resolves** — the journals you find in
life two are the ones you wrote in life one.

**STOLEN — Rogue Legacy + Increlution.** *Mechanic:* a reset carrying a small chosen slice.
*Emotion:* a new run that is **differently shaped**, not merely faster — and the melancholy of
choosing five things out of a life.

## 21.6 The ending

After the **third Legacy**, one more option appears on the Seating, under POUR:

```
        POUR
        STOP
```

**STOP ends the game.** Your final journal is written, the world you last poured continues
without you, and the save becomes a read-only record: every station cleared, the Codex, the
tools you built, and the journals — yours and Sable's, finally in order, indistinguishable. The
last line of the last journal is the first line of the first one you ever found.

You do not have to take it. But the *option* to finish, deliberately, with the game
acknowledging it, is worth more than an infinite tail and it costs one screen.

# 22. HOW THE CHAIN STAYS INVISIBLE (retained, plus the terminal-craft case)

Eight mechanisms, each with the rule that implements it. Unchanged from v1 except 22.9.

**22.1 THE INPUT ARRIVES BEFORE THE MACHINE.** Every machine's primary input begins dropping
**8–20 minutes before the machine exists**, accumulates visibly, and has no use in the
meantime. You had ore, and then something wanted it. Requires the pile to be *visible and
useless* — no auto-conversion, no "you'll need this later" tooltip. A loop the player opened
themselves and the game closed reads as reward; a loop the game opened and closed reads as
homework. **Fires for every machine in the game.**

**22.2 THE NEED IS A GRADIENT; THE GATE IS ONLY ITS LAST STEP.** Hardness rises
*continuously*, so chip time degrades for ten minutes before anything is impassable. By the
time you meet a wall you have wanted a better tool for a while, for reasons of pure friction.
The wall confirms a motivation rather than creating one.

**22.3 THE WALL PRECEDES THE ANSWER; THE READOUT ARRIVES WITH THE MEANS.** A wall you cannot
pass says only **"too hard."** It gains its readout — `hardness 2 · your edge 1` — at the
moment you acquire the capacity to act. `Requires: Hardness 2 Pickaxe` is a shopping list;
nothing is a memory. Same gate, opposite emotion, one string's render timing.

**22.4 EVERY PREREQUISITE HAS TWO REASONS, SHORT ONE FIRST.** You compact cells because they
pay more *right now*; two hours later compaction is the only umberjade source. You chain in
Ferrite because it feels good; later the Coil is the only power. You leave a quadrant fallow
because vines pay; later Sap is the only solvent. **The correct reaction is never "I must go
do this" but "good thing I've been doing this."**

**22.5 SYSTEMS ARRIVE AS BROKEN OBJECTS.** A cracked kiln at depth 9, a seized drill at 28, a
crusher drum with a shattered liner at 47, an assay bench with a snapped needle at 90. An
item in the Hold that does nothing, a REPAIR button, a cost you can already pay. No toast, no
flashing tab. Engaging is curiosity, not compliance — and the shells fill with the equipment
of people who came down before, which states the premise in objects rather than text.

**22.6 THRESHOLDS YOU ARE ALREADY CROSSING.** Unlocks fire on numbers that grow as a side
effect of play — depth record, total refined, cells compacted, chains held — never on a
construction step. And **the threshold is never displayed**: no "??? — 340/500". A visible
meter is an errand again.

**22.7 MANDATORY DESTINATION, OPTIONAL ROUTE.** See 15.3's table. Players who compare notes
find they built the same spine from different parts.

**22.8 THE RHYTHM ALTERNATES.** First instance of any mechanic: **wall first** (teaches the
need). Every later instance: **answer first** (rewards preparation you didn't know you were
doing). Always wall-first is obstructive; always answer-first is a conveyor.

**22.9 THE TERMINAL CRAFT IS A DESTINATION, NOT A TREE (new).** The tension the brief flags
is resolved by showing **the silhouette and none of the path**: seven seat outlines, one
filled, no names, no recipes, no requirement text. The progress meter toward the hundred-hour
goal is the **Breach count**, which the player is advancing anyway. Each seat's requirement
arrives as a *journal fragment at that shell's floor* — after you are standing where you'd
have to be regardless. You can aim at it from hour 12 and you cannot look any of it up, ever.

# 24. THE MOMENT-TO-MOMENT LOOP

**HOUR 1 — TAP / BUY / DESCEND.** Two-to-four second decisions. Pull is pure cadence and it
is enough for exactly this long.

**HOUR 10 — ROUTE / FEED / DISCOVER.** (Ferrite.) Chipping is *routing*: cells are ±, and
chains bank charge into the Coil, which is the only power your refinery has. You are watching
a supply bar while you chip, deciding whether an ore cell on the wrong polarity is worth
breaking a seven-chain for. Between runs you are at the **Alloy Crucible pouring ratios you
guessed**, and about one in four gives you something with a name you have never seen.
Decisions every 5–15 seconds, and a discovery every ten minutes.

**HOUR 40 — RUN THE PLANT.** (Cinder.) You operate a facility whose power source can kill it.
Heat is Draw; more heat is more throughput; too much floods the shaft. You ride 88–93%,
watching a Line finish a six-step pyre batch, deciding whether to vent now and lose 40 seconds
of output or hold ten more seconds. You reorder Draw priority because the Retort is starving
the Crusher. Meanwhile the face runs four stacked signatures at once — a cell can be
compacted, polarised, lit *and* hot, and the right chip order accounts for all four. **Same
6×6 grid as minute zero, obeying four rulesets it did not have then.**

**HOUR 100 — SPEND ATTENTION.** (Spiral.) Six worlds; the five you are not in run at 15%. You
are not mining. You are deciding where to stand, and every minute in one world is a minute the
other five spend at 15%. Between decisions you are at the Seating, holding six of seven parts.

# 25. AUTOMATION — **THE DELEGATION LADDER**

## 25.1 The principle, and the correction

> **Automation never removes decisions. It moves them one level up.**

v4 shipped eleven tiers. The stress test found that four of them (4–7) produced zero moments,
tier 4 was fully subsumed by tier 8, and — the serious one — **the Optimizer and the idle player
converge on the same screen at the fleet era**, in a document that declares itself active-first.

**v5 ships eight tiers, and the late ones generate work instead of absorbing it.**

## 25.2 The eight tiers

Every gate obeys **LAW 9** — a condition or a quality bar, never a repetition count.

| T | Beat | What runs itself | **Earned by (skill, not repetition)** | LAW 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Loam early | **A DRILL** | found at The Undersill | *"The drills are chipping the ore I was saving."* |
| 1 | Loam early | **BEHAVIOURS** — including grain modes | own two drills | *"`Follow` chases my fracture into cells I wanted to keep."* |
| 2 | Loam mid | **THE HOPPER** — a machine pulls its own input | feed a machine **while it is at full demand** 10× | *"The Kiln is eating the graveclay I saved for a Head."* |
| 3 | Ferrite mid | **FILTERS** (Sorting) | sort one mixed stack into three destinations, correctly, once | *"My Crusher filter and my Refinery filter are fighting over the same stock."* |
| 4 | Verdance mid | **THE LINE** — 3–6 machines as one Surge action | Linewright's Fall | *"It runs at its slowest member and wastes Surge on the rest."* |
| 5 | Glassmere late | **THE FOREMAN** — a whole station worked while you are elsewhere | clear one station **without descending** (work it dry) | *"The foreman stripped the seam I was saving to fracture."* |
| 6 | Cinder mid | **THE CIRCUIT** — machines respond to conditions you write, **including world state (§7.3)** | satisfy one Proof using a standing condition | *"My Sieve triggers the Refinery which triggers the Sieve. It's been looping for an hour."* |
| 7 | post-Recursion 2 | **CREWS** — a crewed drift descends on its own | retire a tool that cleared a shell | *"A crew found something only I can resolve."* |
| 8 | post-Recursion 3 | **FLEETS** — several crews, several Rolls | two crews retired safely | *"I have six crews and one of me."* |

**Cut from v4:** standing orders (subsumed by the Circuit), pattern pouring (folded into the
Pattern Bench as a feature), and **the Standing World** — a poured world running your doctrine,
which had no described player action and produced no moment.

## 25.3 THE CIRCUIT

A machine gains a **condition strip**: up to four `WHEN <state> → <action>` rows, evaluated
top-down, drawn from states the machine can see — **and, after §7.3, from the world**.

```
THE REFINERY
  1  WHEN abrasive < 4        → run the Sieve first
  2  WHEN seam = umberjade    → route to the Hold, not the Crusher
  3  WHEN pressure > 88%      → push the band, accept off-spec
  4  WHEN output = flawless   → route to the Caster
```

Four rows is small enough to hold in your head and large enough to produce behaviour you did not
intend, **including loops — which are not a bug.** The Circuit reports them (`row 1 → row 1, 340
times`) and debugging your own plant is one of the most satisfying things a systems game offers.

**Watchable:** the firing row highlights in real time, so a running plant is a column of little
lights moving down a list.

## 25.4 CREWS AND FLEETS — **findings, not logs**

**This is the fix for the third convergence, and it is the most important change in §25.**

A **CREW** is a tool from your rack, a gear loadout and a circuit. Assign it to a drift; it
descends the Roll alone, station by station.

v4 had it report a log. **v5 has it report FINDINGS — and a finding is by construction something
only a human can resolve:**

```
CREW II · held at The Knot
  ▸ WALL, hardness 3 — its tool is 2.            → you, or a better tool
  ▸ A SIGN it cannot read.                        → you, in person
  ▸ A DIG it will not open (circuits don't dig).  → you, in person
  ▸ A seam whose CALL it cannot make.             → you, or leave the double on the table
  ▸ Withdrew from a Deepwrought at 40% condition. → you, or a different loadout
```

**The fleet screen is a queue of work that requires your hands**, and the endgame loop becomes:
dispatch → accumulate findings → **go down there and resolve them personally**. Automation is a
*work-generator*, not a work-eliminator, which is the only version of a late game that is
consistent with an active-first design.

**And crews disagree.** Two crews with different circuits report contradictory findings about
the same station — one says the seam is umberjade, the other says it is not — and the only way
to settle it is to go and look. Cheap to build, and it is a story every time.

**Crews are never destroyed.** A crew that loses withdraws damaged, having dropped its haul.
Never the tool.

**Holy-shit moment:** *"My crew carved through three stations while I slept and left the Curio,
because its circuit doesn't know what those are."* Automation that is *almost* as good as you is
far more interesting than automation that is perfect.

## 25.5 The four automation problems and their levers

1. **It consumes what you were saving.** Lever: filter granularity. **Ship with a RESERVE flag**
   — any stack marked reserved in one tap is untouchable. If playtest shows people reserving
   everything, filters are too coarse.
2. **It decides differently than you would.** Lever: how far a circuit can see. **This should
   stay slightly painful** — a line that never annoys you is one you have no reason to improve.
3. **Scale needs infrastructure.** A Foreman costs Flow *continuously*, so automating more means
   generating more, which means playing the shell's signature act more. **Automation feeds back
   into the most active layer in the game.**
4. **You stop knowing your own plant.** At tier 6+ the Circuit's log is the only record. That is
   deliberate, and it is why the log is written in plain sentences.

# 26. THE RECORD — *identity, one track*

v4 had three identity systems (marks h6, archetypes h25, doctrines h124) for <1% of playtime,
one readout, and **an internal contradiction with LAW 3.** v5 collapses them into one hardening
track and fixes the contradiction.

## 26.1 Five stats, and every threshold is a capability

| Stat | Sample thresholds |
|---|---|
| **HAND** | **3** cut figures · **6** figures on compacted rock · **9** a chip crits through one hardness band |
| **WIND** | **4** SWEEP costs half · **7** two stances per exchange · **10** Wind refreshes mid-Standoff |
| **NERVE** | **4** work at 85% pressure · **7** the Silence no longer blinds · **10** hazard rules at half strength |
| **SIGHT** | **3** see a station's seam unprospected · **6** **see two intents ahead** · **9** see a material's hidden trait · **12** read a strain's mutation before it fires |
| **GRIP** | **4** a second tool on the rack · **7** two Charm slots · **10** carry a live strain between quadrants |

**SIGHT 6 is no longer a tax.** In v4 it bought the ability to see *the* intent, which meant
combat was a coinflip until you paid — the stress test correctly compared this to Slay the Spire
selling back the mechanic's load-bearing half. **In v5 intent is free from the first fight
(§27), and SIGHT 6 buys the second intent ahead** — a luxury that makes you better, not a toll
that makes the early game bad.

## 26.2 MARKS — **hidden until earned** *(LAW 3 restored)*

A mark is a permanent identity trait earned by how you have actually played. **You cannot see
the list.** A mark arrives unannounced, named, describing something you did without meaning to.

**Six marks, down from nine.** The three cut (`Cold`, `Overdrawn`, `Wanderer`) were modifiers
wearing an identity's clothes.

| Mark | Earned by | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Ashwalker** | working hazard stations habitually | hazard rules apply one step later |
| **Patient** | taking cells to the lock threshold repeatedly | compaction +25%, chip speed −10% |
| **Thrifty** | reaching a floor having never salvaged | salvage returns designs as well as material |
| **Unfed** | clearing a WALL owning zero drills | hand chips count double toward Proofs |
| **Sure-Handed** | cutting figures habitually | a figure may be cut mid-Standoff |
| **Read-Deep** | proving propositions habitually | a Proof may be satisfied by a crew |

**v4's LAW 5 sentence is cut** — *"I want Ashwalker for the Choke"* requires foreknowledge the
player cannot have. **The replacement:** *"I have five marks and three slots and every one of
them is something I did without noticing."*

**The panel is a mirror**, which is the claim v4 made and could not keep. Now it can.

## 26.3 HARDENING — archetypes and the commitment, merged

The three-layer ladder becomes **one track with three hardenings:**

1. **MARKS** arrive as you play. Many, swappable, hidden until earned.
2. **AN ARCHETYPE** is recognised when your marks and stats cross a pattern — the game *names*
   you: *"You have been mining like a Breaker."* One at a time, soft, re-qualifiable.
3. **THE COMMITMENT** is available at Recursion 1: your archetype hardens permanently, gains a
   second capability, and **survives Recursion.** One, costly.

**Six archetypes, and under LAW 8 each is strong in a different pillar — so which one you want
depends on which Seat you are chasing this Recursion, and that changes every Recursion by
design (§4).**

| Archetype | Capability | Strongest when chasing |
|---|---|---|
| **The Breaker** | deep-entry drops one compaction tier earlier | Seats I, V (compaction, heat) |
| **The Wright** | machine parts swap without re-casting | Seats II, IV (plant-heavy) |
| **The Reader** | a proposition opens with a harder Proof instead of notes | any floor you are brute-forcing |
| **The Furnaceman** | the flood threshold is a decision, not a constant | Seat V, and all flooding (§36) |
| **The Grafter** | a strain carries two traits | Seat III |
| **The Quartermaster** | one extra crew, forever | the fleet era |

**Holy-shit moment:** the game names something you did not know you were doing.
**Fun at zero bonuses: yes** — being described accurately by a game you have played for a day is
a strong feeling and it is nearly free to build.

# 27. COMBAT — **THE STANDOFF**, deepened

The stress test's verdict on v4's combat: *deterministic rules + six fixed options + one enemy =
a lookup table solved by the third encounter*, with **three live stances of six** and a stat tax
in front of it. The choice was DEEPEN or DEMOTE.

**v5 DEEPENS.** Three changes, and they are the whole fix.

## 27.1 Change one — intent is free, from the first fight

No SIGHT requirement. You always see what it will do next. **STOLEN — Slay the Spire, properly
this time.** *Mechanic:* intent telegraphs. *Emotion:* **you always know, and you can still be
wrong** — which is only true if knowing is free and the difficulty is elsewhere.

## 27.2 Change two — **two actors**

You bring a **DRILL LINE** into the fight: a drill behaviour set *before* the Standoff, which
acts automatically every exchange and **cannot be changed mid-fight** except once.

So a Standoff is your hand playing *around* a machine you configured in advance:

| Your drill line | Does, every exchange |
|---|---|
| **Fullest-first** | steady chip damage |
| **Sweep** | small damage to the thing *and* strips one stack of whatever it is accumulating |
| **Adjacency chain** | damage that grows while you do not interrupt it |
| **Follow (grain)** | nothing for two exchanges, then a large hit |
| **Marked** | does nothing until you PRY, then everything at once |

**This is the second axis.** The question stops being *which button* and becomes *did I bring the
right machine, and can I play my hand around what it is doing.* It also welds the Drill Bay — a
pure economy system in v4 — into combat, and makes grain modes (§2.4) matter twice.

## 27.3 Change three — **it reads your last stance**

A Deepwrought that sees the same stance twice in a row **adapts**: the repeated stance is halved
and its next intent counters it. **Repetition is punished by construction**, which is what kills
the lookup table. You cannot solve a fight once and perform it forever; you have to keep moving.

## 27.4 The six stances — all live now

| Stance | From | Does |
|---|---|---|
| **STRIKE** | tool chip power | damage |
| **GUARD** | mantle | reduce incoming, convert some to Wind |
| **PRY** | tool Edge | **cancel its next intent** (not "double the next strike" — v4's version was arithmetically identical to striking twice) |
| **VENT** | harness | dump accumulated hazard — survival in Cinder and Hollow |
| **FEED** | glove | **re-command your drill line, once per Standoff** |
| **WITHDRAW** | always | leave; keep what you took, lose the drop |

READ is **cut** (intent is free). FEED replaces it and is the interesting one: one mid-fight
override, and *when* you spend it is the fight's central decision.

## 27.5 Intents are drawn from the shell's signature

| Shell | Its Deepwrought | The exam |
|---|---|---|
| **Loam** | **COMPACTS** — hardens each exchange: less damage taken, richer drop | how long do you keep hitting something getting tougher and richer? |
| **Ferrite** | **POLARISES** — intents alternate ±; a matching stance doubles, an opposing one nullifies | the chain, as a fight |
| **Verdance** | **GROWS** — strengthens if you do not act, drop scales with size | the fallow decision, with teeth |
| **Glassmere** | **REFRACTS** — vulnerable only in bands you have allocated | your Spectrum is your weapon |
| **Cinder** | **PRESSURISES** — every exchange raises your heat; venting costs a turn | the ride |
| **Hollow** | **UNDECIDED** — intent unknown until you commit; naming it fixes it | witnessing, as a fight |
| **Aleph** | **CITES** — a different shell's rule each exchange | all of it |

**Hollow is the one shell where intent is not free**, and that is the point — it is the shell of
absence, and paying a Witness to see is its whole grammar.

## 27.6 The Wardens — the boss *is* the floor gate

| Warden | Floor | The exam | Drops |
|---|---|---|---|
| **THE TAPMOTHER** | Deepgrave | compacts *your whole face* — every cell hardens each exchange | first **Deepgrave** · a Curio |
| **THE LOADSTAR** | Poleiron | flips your polarity each exchange; banked Surge is your damage pool | **Poleiron** |
| **OLD PLENTY** | Heartwood | grows while you do not hit it; the fight is deciding when to stop letting it | **Heartwood** |
| **THE UNSEEN ANGLE** | Truelight | exists only in unallocated bands; you re-tune the Spectrum mid-exchange | **Truelight** |
| **THE SMOLDER** | Slagglass | it *is* the pressure — striking raises heat, venting damages you | **Slagglass** |
| **WHAT IS LEFT** | Nought | no intent until you commit, unless you spend a Witness | **Nought** |
| **THE AUTHOR** | The Core | every signature, one per exchange, ordered by your own play history | **Record** |

**Each Warden drops the first sample of that shell's Seat material**, so combat is welded to the
terminal craft rather than bolted beside it.

## 27.7 Danger as texture

Hazard stations show their Deepwrought on the Roll before you arrive; engaging is optional, the
station's material usually is not. **LAIRS** (2 per shell) are opt-in and drop gear linings
available nowhere else. **Nothing is lost permanently:** losing costs your haul and your Wind and
sends you up one station. Preparation always works.

## 27.8 Phases

`survive one` → `read the drill line as a second actor` → `build gear for a specific shell` →
`stance loadouts as builds` → `Wardens as exams` → **`the Reflected`**: Deepwrought whose intents
are drawn from your own archetype and marks. The last phase is the fight against the way you
play — the only content authored by your save file.

**Honest note on the Reflected:** the stress test flagged that a generic version is worse than
its absence. **It ships only if the generator can produce a fight a player recognises as
themselves within three exchanges.** If it cannot, cut it — do not ship a Deepwrought with a
themed name.

**LAW 5:** *"I can beat anything in Ferrite and a Verdance lair took me apart, because
everything I own is built for chains."*

# 28. GEAR

Three parts (Body · Lining · Fitting) rather than the tool's seven — a lighter craft you do more
often, through machinery that already exists.

| Slot | Governs | Shell interaction |
|---|---|---|
| **LAMP** | how many cells show grain and state | Hollow: a `hollow` lamp shows an undecided stack's distribution · Glassmere: a `keen` lamp adds a seventh band |
| **MANTLE** | GUARD strength; hazard protection | Cinder: `warm` raises the flood ceiling · `rime` slows pressure gain and slows you |
| **HARNESS** | carry; VENT; drifts held open | Verdance: `springy` carries a live strain between quadrants |
| **BOOTS** | descent cost; hazard damage floor | Loam: `dense` reduces compaction loss when you leave a cell |
| **GLOVE** | chip speed; figure precision; **FEED** strength | Ferrite: `charged` holds a chain through one break |
| **CHARM ×2** | relics, runes, gems, Curios | anything |

**A loadout is a build.** The Ferrite kit (charged glove, keen lamp) is a different game from the
Cinder kit (warm mantle, rime boots).

**Swapping now costs a station.** v4 let you swap in one tap, which the stress test correctly
called a solved reflex rather than a decision. **You may change loadout only at a REST station or
on Collapse** — so what you carry into a stretch of the Roll is a commitment you make in advance,
and a crew's loadout is fixed for its whole descent.

**LAW 5:** *"Every piece of gear I want costs the same flawless stock as the tool part I also
want."*

# 29. CULTIVATION AND ALCHEMY — **THE STRAIN**

The only system that plays out across resets.

## 29.1 The loop

```
mine a trait-bearing seam    →  the quadrant remembers what you took
leave it fallow              →  a STRAIN sprouts carrying that trait
let it stand through Collapse →  it MATURES one stage (3 stages)
cross two mature strains     →  a hybrid, or a MUTATION
harvest                      →  Sap (the solvent) + live trait-bearing stock
distil that stock            →  an essence no ore in the shell carries
infuse it onto a metal       →  an alloy that was impossible
that alloy makes a drill head →  a drill BEHAVIOUR that did not exist
that drill works a seam      →  whose ore starts this loop with a better trait
```

## 29.2 The mechanic, trimmed

Four quadrant rows with a stage bar and a trait list. A strain matures **one stage per Collapse
survived** — the reset becomes a clock you want to advance. **Crossing** two stage-3 strains
gives a hybrid carrying both traits, or **at ~15% a MUTATION**: a trait neither parent had, drawn
from the shell you are *currently in*.

**The kind of thinking is breeding under uncertainty with a long feedback loop**, and nothing
else in the document asks you to plan three resets ahead.

**Trimmed from v4 (§43):** per-shell inheritance tables cut; mutation draws from the current
shell's trait pool rather than an authored per-pairing table. **~60 rows reclaimed**, and the
mechanic is unchanged from the player's side.

## 29.3 What it produces that nothing else does

**Sap** (Verdance's only solvent — the Washer runs on your gardening) · **live stock**, the only
material that can be distilled *without losing its other traits*, which is the Still's late
supply and the reason essence work never becomes a grind · **mutations**, four of which are
required for tier XIII+ parts.

## 29.4 Alchemy — the Reaction Bench's other half

Cultivation and the Reaction Bench (§17) are **one pillar** because they answer the same
question — *how do I get a material the world does not contain?* — by two routes: **grow it** or
**react it**. Keeping them separate would fail the uniqueness test. The Bench supplies
**growth reagents** that shorten a maturation by one Collapse, which is the most wanted
consumable in the middle game.

**LAW 5:** *"My best strain is one Collapse from stage three and I need to Collapse now to afford
the stair."* **Tuning lever: whether an early Collapse costs a stage or merely fails to advance
one.** Ship the softer version.

# 30. ARCHAEOLOGY — **THE DIG**

**14 DIG stations across the Roll** — places where somebody's plant was buried when a shell
shifted. A dig is not mining; it is a separate panel with different rules.

A site has **LAYERS** (4–9, count unknown) and you have a **budget of STRIKES** (12–20, set by
HAND and your glove). Each strike reveals one layer and *damages* what is beneath. After each
layer you get a **read** — three adjectives (`iron fittings · scorched · aligned`) — and infer.
**You choose when to stop.**

**STOLEN — Return of the Obra Dinn's inference-from-partial-evidence, plus press-your-luck
stopping.** *Mechanic:* evidence accumulating toward a commitment. *Emotion:* **being almost
sure, and choosing anyway.**

| Depth | Typically |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | material, common — the consolation |
| 3–5 | **a machine part from a tier you cannot yet build** |
| 6–7 | **a Curio** |
| 8+ | **a blueprint** — a named tool design you did not author |
| bottom, **~4 sites in the whole game** | **a WORKING MACHINE**, intact, usable now |

The working-machine bottom is rate-limited hard and hand-placed: it is the best moment in the
pillar and it trivialises the plant if it happens twice.

**Blueprints are the deep hook** — a stranger's design, named, using a material you have never
seen, is a want you did not have thirty seconds earlier. At the Legacy (§21.5) **they are your
own**, from a previous life, found in the WORKS stations your last plant left behind.

**Crews excavate badly:** a crew always strikes to the bottom, so it brings back broken things —
which is a genuine reason to go yourself and the healthiest failure mode automation has.

**LAW 5:** *"Four strikes left, the read says something aligned two layers down, and if I'm
wrong I've wasted the site."*

## 31.1 Seven units, each forced by a structure

| Beat | Unit | Thinking about | Forced by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loam early | **the cell** | which cell, with or across the grain | — |
| Loam late | **the run** | which station do I stop at | the Roll; drills take cells off your desk |

| Glassmere | **the build** | what kind of delver am I | marks → archetype; loadouts |

| post-Recursion 3 | **the fleet** | where do my hands go | crews, and their findings |
| the Seating | **the inheritance** | what of this survives me | the Legacy |

Each **retires** the last as a place where decisions live.

## 31.2 THE CASTING FLOOR — trimmed to its core

The stress test found it *massively over-designed for ~2% of playtime*: compatibility grammar,
nine defects, stability budgets, exhaustion timers, forecasting and underwriting, for a system
arriving after the first Recursion.

**v5 ships two things: band assignment and one defect.**

```
BANDS    0–40    Cinder physics
         41–90   Loam physics
         91–150  Hollow physics
DEFECT   "every wall is one hardness higher"   ← mandatory, one of six
```

You **POUR** it and it becomes a live world with its own generated Roll and its physics in the
wrong places.

**Why you do it, unchanged:** Seats V, VI and VII require conditions no natural shell provides.
Slagglass needs the Retort at 96% pressure *and* a Loam-depth descent cost, and those never
co-occur naturally. **The last three Seats are only obtainable from a world you specified.**

**Kept:** band compatibility is *discovered* (Hollow cannot sit above Verdance — absence needs
something to be absent from), and learning the grammar is the endgame's Codex.

**Cut (§43):** stability budgets, exhaustion timers, yield forecasting, **the Underwrite**, and
three of nine defects — including *"no journals,"* which the document itself identified as
free and therefore dominant.

**The six defects that remain all cost something mechanical**, and **the list offered is drawn
from systems you actually use** — so a plant-heavy player is offered `Draw is halved` and a
face-heavy player is offered `every wall is one hardness higher`. **A defect you don't care
about is never on the menu.**

**Reclaimed: ~90 rows**, for a system that keeps its entire reason to exist.

# 34. THE LOOPS

Where the tail feeds the head — the cycles, not the dependency graph.

**L1 · THE ASH LOOP** — `Kiln → Ash → Char → better fuel → more Brick → bigger field → more Dust
→ Kiln`. The first byproduct that feeds its own producer, noticed around minute 25.

**L2 · THE PLANT LOOP** — `Forge → cast parts → build a machine → it refines material → better
parts → better machine`, closed at the far end by **trait transmission (§7.4)**: your machine's
parts now flavour what it makes, so improving the plant changes what the Forge has to work with.

**L3 · THE UNDERSTANDING LOOP** — `do something novel → notes → a proposition → a new capability
inside a system you own → that capability enables a novel act → notes`. The only loop fuelled by
your own inventiveness.

**L4 · THE COMBAT LOOP** — `mine → gear → survive a deeper hazard → deeper stations → Deepwrought
drops → gear linings that exist nowhere else → work hazards you could not → whose seams feed the
tool`.

**L5 · THE GARDEN LOOP** — `mine a trait → fallow → strain → mature across Collapses → cross →
mutation → distil → infuse → alloy → drill head → new drill behaviour → works a seam → whose ore
is the trait you started with, better`.

**L6 · THE ARCHAEOLOGY LOOP** — `dig → Curio → seated in a machine → it behaves differently → a
material you could not make → gear → survive a deeper hazard station → which has a dig site → …`
and at the Legacy it closes completely: **your own plant becomes the thing you dig up.**

**L7 · THE RESET LOOP** — `descend → Cores → capacity → longer chains → purity → tool tier →
deeper → Seat → Breach → the signature carries down → the face itself changes → descend
differently`.

**L8 · THE WORLD LOOP** — `play → Seats → the Seating → pour a world → exotic materials no
natural shell holds → better plant and tools → deeper Seats` → and at the Legacy, **your
inheritance changes minute one of the next life.**

**L9 · THE HEAT LOOP (new, §36)** — `flood a station → it becomes hot and rich forever → working
it leaks heat into your machines (§7.2) → warped machines behave differently → which changes what
your plant produces → which changes what you can afford to flood next`.

**Chains with no return path, named:** the Balance (fixed — the worth ledger feeds the Assay
Call) · Figures (fixed — they are the primary note source) · Secrets (accepted; a secret that
feeds a system is a puzzle). **Machine levels was the fourth and it is now cut.**

# 36. THE BACK HALF — the two dead zones, filled

The stress test found two stretches producing almost nothing: **late Cinder** (pressure-riding at
increasing numbers) and **the fleet era** (thirty-six hours, four moments, three of them
variations on *"my crew did something without me"*). Both are content-density problems and both
are fixable with systems that already exist.

## 36.1 LATE CINDER — **OVERPRESSURE WORK**

**What early Cinder does:** you survive pressure.
**What late Cinder now does:** you *spend* it — on the world.

### THE FLOODGATE ★ *(found at a late Cinder station; replaces nothing, merges two v4 machines)*

You may deliberately **FLOOD a station** — not the run, a station. It costs a full heat bank and
it is **irreversible**.

A flooded station, permanently and through every Collapse:

1. **Its seam re-rolls to Cinder-native stock** — pyre, obsidian, Slagglass precursors — and
   stops re-rolling thereafter. It is now a reliable source of the shell's best material.
2. **It becomes a HAZARD station forever.** You cannot work it without heat gear; a crew without
   a `rime` mantle will not enter it.
3. **Its grain collapses to a single direction.** A flooded station is the best place in the game
   to drive a fracture wave (§2), because every cell points the same way.
4. **Its heat leaks into any machine working there** (§7.2) — so a plant run at a flooded station
   warps faster, in both directions.

### What this produces

**You are terraforming the Roll.** Flooding three adjacent stations makes a **heat corridor**: a
stretch of the shaft that is permanently hot, permanently rich, permanently grain-aligned, and
permanently hostile to anything not built for it. It is the first time the player changes the
world rather than passing through it, and **it is the grammar lesson for the Casting Floor** —
you learn to edit a world before you are allowed to pour one.

**Stories it produces, none of them authored:**
- *"I flooded the Ashfall and now I can't work it without the rime mantle I sold."*
- *"I built a heat corridor from 300 to 340 and my crews won't go down it."*
- *"I flooded a station with a Dig site in it and I don't know if that was clever or stupid."*
- *"My whole plant runs warped because I keep working the corridor."*

**LAW 5:** *"I made the richest station in the shell and locked myself out of it."*

**Cost:** one machine, one station flag, one interaction with existing gear and hazard rules.
**Late Cinder goes from one verb to a verb that changes the map.**

## 36.2 THE FLEET ERA — **findings become work**

The mechanism is in §25.4: crews report **findings**, and a finding is by construction something
only a human can resolve. This is what that produces as *content*.

### The resolution queue

The fleet screen is not a log. It is a **queue of jobs that need your hands**, and each has a
shape:

| Finding | What resolving it is |
|---|---|
| **A wall it cannot pass** | go down and break it, or re-tool the crew |
| **A Sign it cannot read** | go pursue it yourself — crews cannot investigate |
| **A Dig it will not open** | go excavate; crews always strike to the bottom and break things |
| **A seam whose Call it cannot make** | go call it, or leave the double on the table |
| **A Deepwrought it withdrew from** | go fight it, or send a different loadout |
| **A contradiction** | two crews disagree about the same station — **go and look** |
| **A drift into somewhere you have never been** | crews can open drifts you have not walked, so they find stations *you* have not seen |

**That last row is the fleet era's real content.** A crew is not a farm; it is a **scout**. It
opens ground and hands you the parts it cannot do, and the parts it cannot do are precisely the
interesting ones — investigation, deduction, gambling, and fighting.

### Why this fixes the third convergence

The stress test's most serious structural finding was that the Optimizer and the idle player
**arrive at the same screen** at the fleet era, in a document that declares itself active-first.

They no longer do. The idle player is not here — they stopped at Ferrite (§0.4), and that is a
stated decision. And the Optimizer's fleet does not *replace* his play; it **generates a work
queue he must personally clear**, which is the opposite of an idle screen. **Late-game automation
in v5 produces active play rather than absorbing it.**

**LAW 5:** *"I have six crews and eleven findings and one of me, and three of the findings are
things I actually want to go do."*

**Partially.** Of 53 verbs, **20 stay new for an hour or more** and **16 are learned in half an

# 45. RISKS, AND WHAT CANNOT BE KNOWN WITHOUT PLAYING

## 45.1 The risks

1. **Grain could be fiddly rather than tactical.** Four directions on every cell of a growing
   grid is a lot of information at 380px. **Mitigation: the grain tick is the only new pixel, and
   drills can be set to handle grain for you.** If it reads as noise, the fallback is grain per
   *band* rather than per cell — coarser, still directional, far less to parse.
2. **Combat is still the largest bet.** Two actors and adaptation are real depth on paper. If the
   Standoff panel is not fun at two stances and one drill line, the Gap-3 answer collapses and
   Wardens should become non-combat exams. **Build it fifth and find out.**
3. **The re-roll could make the Roll feel arbitrary.** If a station's contents change too much,
   the name stops meaning anything and the ladder loses the legibility that justified it.
   **Lever: how wide the re-roll band is.** Start narrow.
4. **E1 could compound to nonsense.** Four cap-and-decay rules are specified (§7.4) and they are
   guesses. Prototype on three machines.
5. **Findings could become a chore queue.** If resolving a finding is not interesting *in
   itself*, the fleet era becomes a to-do list — which is what it was in v4, wearing a better
   name. The seven finding types must each be a thing a player would choose to do.
6. **Flow/Surge could just be two sliders.** It only produces different plants if machine demand
   profiles genuinely differ. If they cluster, it is one number with extra steps.
7. **The idle player is gone after Loam** (§0.4). Stated as a decision, and it costs an audience.
8. **Draw remains a choke point.** Supply generous, capacity tight. Challenge #1 is the safety
   valve.
9. **The bootstrap can deadlock.** Extend `reachability.test.ts` to walk the tier graph and
   assert every tier is reachable from the one below. Highest-value test in the project.
10. **Authoring load is still ~1,260 rows** even after cutting 1,283. It is a multi-year build.

## 45.2 What cannot be known without building

1. **Whether compaction and grain are fun to perform five hundred times.** 28% of playtime runs
   through one panel. No further design settles this — chip for two hours and find out.
2. **Whether notes-by-novelty is an economy or a dead end.** Softened (propositions are off floor
   gates, §10.2) but not eliminated. Only real players running out of novelty will show it.
3. **Whether the Roll survives its eighth pass through a shell.** §1.1's re-roll is the answer
   and it is an argument, not a measurement.
4. **Whether E1 produces a house style or a mess.** The only genuinely unpredictable system in
   the document is unpredictable to its designer too. That is the point and also the risk.
5. **Whether a resolution queue reads as agency or as chores.** The difference is entirely in
   how the seven finding types feel to clear, and that cannot be designed further — only built.
6. **Whether the moment density is too high, particularly in Loam.** §58.2 counts moments and
   treats more as better. **It never asks whether they arrive too close together.** Loam opens 19
   verbs and ~35 moments in ~5 hours, and surprise has diminishing returns — the game may need
   deliberately quiet stretches (mining, optimising, building, nothing new) to make discoveries
   land. **This is a tuning number, not a design change**, and it can only be found by playing the
   opening.

**These six should not receive another design pass. They should receive code.**

The stress test's verdict was that v4 described **an excellent 20-hour game inside a 200-hour

> **A 42-hour game, of which ~20 hours are authored content that is consumed once, ~19 are

# 47. THE SEVEN ACCIDENTAL DISCOVERIES

LAW 10's corollary. **One per shell. Not in the Codex count. Not hinted on the Roll. Not
reachable by brute-forcing a menu.** Each is something a player does *by accident, by laziness,
or by misjudgement* — which is why they will be found, and why finding one feels like the world
having a secret rather than the game having a checklist.

| Shell | What you do | What happens | Why it will be found |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LOAM** | Take one cell to compaction 26 **entirely with the grain**, never once across | It does not lock. It **opens** — a shaft to the band below, free, permanent, no `dustCost` | Players who are afraid of locking cells will chip with the grain out of caution and eventually overshoot |
| **FERRITE** | **MARK a cell, then break your chain on it** | The Coil banks the chain anyway, once per Collapse — a broken chain that pays | Everyone breaks chains constantly, and everyone marks cells; the overlap is inevitable |
| **VERDANCE** | **Never mine one quadrant for an entire arc** | It does not just grow — it produces a **Heartwood sapling**, the Seat III material, an entire shell early | Half of all players will ignore a corner of the face for an hour without meaning to |
| **GLASSMERE** | **Allocate zero intensity to every band** | The face goes dark and unchippable — **and the Deepwrought cannot see you.** Hazard stations become walkable | Everybody zeroes a slider to see what it does |
| **CINDER** | **Do not vent until the gauge reads exactly 100** | The flood happens, is survived, and the station is **flooded for free** — no Floodgate, no heat bank | Misjudging the gauge is the most common mistake in the shell |
| **HOLLOW** | **Never observe one stack for an entire Recursion** | It becomes **a Jar of Nothing** — the Curio, generated by you rather than found | Hoarders do this by temperament; so does anyone who forgets a stack exists |
| **ALEPH** | **CITE a technique onto a cell of its own shell** — which should be pointless | It doubles | Fumbling a targeting UI on a six-physics grid |

**None of these is required for anything.** They are the only content in the document a player can
finish the game without ever meeting, and that is deliberate: **LAW 10 is about the existence of
things you were not told about**, and a secret that everyone is funnelled into is a tutorial.

**Two of the seven are "something that only happens if you *don't* do an obvious thing"**
(Verdance, Hollow), which is the hardest shape to discover and the most satisfying to hear about.

# 48. THE COLLECTION LAYER — things you want because they are cool

**The biggest single gap v5 had.** Everything in it was instrumental. This layer is objects with
identity, several of which do nothing at all, and the test for every one is **fun at zero
bonuses** — would a player be pleased to own it if it had no stats.

## 48.1 THE DEAD — the spine of the whole layer · **INVENTED**

**Twelve named delvers came down before you.** Each left **two to four objects**, scattered by
depth, and each **stopped somewhere.**

```
KELL'S LAMP
  found at Umberdeep, Loam 90
  "Kell went down with three others and came back alone twice."
  — a lamp. It shows grain one band further than yours. It is not better than
    what you can build by Ferrite.
```

You find Kell's lamp in Loam, Kell's glove in Ferrite, Kell's blueprint in Verdance — **and then
nothing, ever, anywhere.** Kell stopped at Verdance. You learn that by the absence.

Some of the twelve got further than you are. **Finding a delver's last object tells you where
they died, and finding one deeper than you have been tells you someone was there first.**

**This is a story engine that costs a name table.** Twelve delvers × 3 objects = 36 authored
items, and the *provenance line on each* is the content. It also makes the Roll personal: a
station is not "depth 90," it is "where Kell's lamp was."

**And at the Legacy (§21.5), you become the thirteenth.** Your objects scatter into the next
life's Roll with your provenance line, generated from your own record. Sable was always a
previous you; **now so is everybody else.**

## 48.2 The ten categories

| Category | What it is | Where | Does it do anything? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ANCIENT CASTS** | a cast someone else poured, at a tolerance you cannot match — **and three of them are for part shapes the Tool Station has no slot for** | deep Digs, Chambers, WORKS | mostly yes; **the three impossible ones do nothing at all** and are the best items in the game |
| **PROTOTYPE DRILL CORES** | abandoned designs the Bay cannot make: one that mines *backwards* (fills cells), one that only works on **locked** cells, one that follows grain across station boundaries | Digs, LAIRS | yes, strangely — several are strictly worse and wanted anyway |
| **LEGENDARY ALLOYS** | named, with provenance, **not among the 60** — and unrepeatable because one input was a material state that no longer exists | one-time events, the Dead's kits | yes, and you have the only one |
| **NAMED RELICS** | the Dead's gear, with provenance (§48.1) | scattered by depth | modest — provenance is the point |
| **LOST BLUEPRINTS** | a *named* delver's tool design, buildable | Digs layer 8+ | yes — building someone else's tool |
| **FORGOTTEN MACHINE VARIANTS** | a machine built wrong that works differently — **an evolution branch (§50) you did not unlock** | Digs, WORKS | yes, and it teaches you an evolution exists |
| **HISTORICAL TOOL DESIGNS** | **Sable's seven tools**, one per shell, reconstructable from journal fragments | the journals themselves | yes — and completing all seven is the game's quietest long goal |
| **UNIQUE RUNE PHRASES** | sequences outside the rune grammar, found written on things rather than in a menu | wall margins, tool engravings, journals | yes, and they cannot be derived |
| **IMPOSSIBLE MATERIALS** | things that should not exist: a material with **five traits** when the cap is four | one-time events, secrets | yes, once — and then it is gone |
| **PERFECT SPECIMENS** | `aberrant` purity rolls uniform 1–100 (`materials.ts`). **A specimen at exactly 100 is a 1-in-100 you chose not to spend.** | anywhere, by luck | **no.** It is a material you are keeping instead of using, which is the entire point |
| **THE FIRST OF EVERYTHING** | the game quietly keeps your first tool, first alloy, first Curio, first slip, first flooded station. You cannot get another | automatic | **no.** Pure sentiment, free to build |

**Perfect Specimens and the First of Everything are the two that prove the layer works**, because
both are literally *the absence of use.* A player who keeps a purity-100 graveclay for two hundred
hours instead of pouring it has been given something a stat could not give them.

## 48.3 THE LONG SHELF — how you live with them

v5 cut the Cabinet because it traded four rule-changes for one and no constant made both sides
live. **v6 does not repeat that trade and does not pretend to solve it — it removes it.**

> **The Long Shelf is a RECORD, not a location.** Finding a collectible enters it on the Shelf
> permanently. **Using the object does not remove it from the Shelf.** Display costs nothing
> because what is displayed is *the fact that you found it*, not the object.

**Purely cosmetic, and owned as such.** There is no bonus for a full row, no set completion
effect, no rule granted. It is the wall you look at.

What stops it being a list: **it is dense, it is ordered by where you found things, and it is the
game's screenshot** (§56). A Shelf at 300 hours has thirty-six objects belonging to twelve dead
people, seven of Sable's tools, three casts for parts that do not exist, and a graveclay at
purity 100 that you have never used.

**Twelve tests:** skip **yes — and that is correct for this layer, it is the one thing in the
document that is optional by design** · deletion breaks **the Dead's provenance stories, §56's
visible progression, three secrets that reward you with collectibles, and the Legacy's
you-become-one-of-them beat** · fun at zero bonuses **— it is the definition** · watchable **yes,
that is its only job** · placed **everywhere, weighted deep**.

## 49.2 HOLLOW'S LIFT — *without adding a verb or a machine*

§58.2's thinnest beats are Hollow, Aleph and the ending. **Hollow's problem is not a lack of
systems — its identity is subtraction, and more mechanics damage it.** Its problem is a lack of
*"what the hell was that?"*

So the fix spends rows reclaimed in §59 on **surprise, not machinery**:

- **Two new secrets** (16, 17 above), both Hollow, both using the Witness and the Reconstruction
  Frame exactly as they already are.
- **A rebuilt cell keeps its chosen physics through Collapse.** You are assembling a permanent
  face out of nothing, one cell per arc — which makes the Frame a *long* project rather than a
  panel, at the cost of one persistence flag.
- **Witnessing at maximum Silence names the stack *and something adjacent*** — a second thing
  resolves that you did not point at, and you do not get to choose what.

**~10 rows, no new verb, no new machine.** Hollow's moment count rises from 13 to 17 (§60.2),
and every addition is the shell being *stranger*, not busier.

**Aleph is not lifted and I am saying so:** it is forty depths and a composite exam by design
(§0.5.3). Giving it more would make it a shell it is not.

**Design rule enforced across all fifteen:** none requires a specific *sequence of many steps*.
Every one is a single unusual act, or the *absence* of an ordinary one. That is what makes them
stumble-into-able rather than brute-forceable, and it is why they will actually be found and
shared rather than datamined.

# 50. MACHINE EVOLUTION — a machine becomes what you fed it

v5 has machine **tiers** (capability unlocks) and cut machine **levels** (dead numbers). What it
lacked was machines developing an **identity**.

**This is not machine levels.** There is no ladder, no purchase, and no number. There is one
irreversible branch, chosen by **how you used the machine**, and you get it once.

## 50.1 The trigger — its DIET

A machine keeps a rolling record of what it has processed. When a machine at tier III or above
has run a **strongly-skewed diet** for a sustained stretch — roughly three-quarters of its
throughput carrying one trait, or one purity band, or one source — it **offers to evolve**, once,
into the branch its diet points at.

**LAW 9 compliant:** the gate is a *condition* (feed it a coherent diet), not a count. And it has
a real cost — **a coherent diet is rarely the efficient one**, because efficiency means feeding a
machine whatever is nearest. Choosing to specialise a machine means deliberately routing around
your own convenience for a stretch, using Sorting filters to do it.

**It is irreversible.** A machine you have used for twenty hours becomes a specific thing forever,
and that is the point.

## 50.2 The branches — eight machines, three each

| Machine | Fed… | Becomes | What changes, permanently |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CRUSHER** | `keen` stock | **PULVERIZER** | output is already fine — skips the Mill step entirely; **no byproduct at all** |
| | `dense` stock | **COMPRESSOR** | byproduct doubled; markedly slower |
| | trait-varied stock | **RESONANCE CRUSHER** | output **inherits the input's dominant trait** — E1 (§7.4) turned up to full strength in one machine |
| **REFINERY** | high-band stock | **THE POLISHER** | can exceed a band by one on a clean cycle |
| | tailings and low-band | **THE SALVAGER** | refines **directly from tailings**, skipping the Washer |
| | mixed bands | **THE SORTER** | outputs two bands **separately** instead of averaging |
| **KILN** | Char | **THE BANKED HEARTH** | stores heat **across Collapse** |
| | raw Dust | **THE FAST BURN** | output becomes pure **Surge** |
| | its own Ash | **THE GREY KILN** | makes **Grey Char** on demand (secret #2, industrialised) |
| **STILL** | one trait repeatedly | **THE SPECIALIST** | that trait's vials double; every other trait halves |
| | many traits | **THE BROAD STILL** | two essences per unit at reduced potency |
| | live strain stock | **THE LIVING COLUMN** | distils **without consuming** the source |
| **CASTER** | one part shape | **THE MOULDWRIGHT** | that shape pours at ancient-cast tolerance |
| | many shapes | **THE JOURNEYMAN** | any pour may substitute one missing part at a coherence cost |
| | unstable stock | **THE ROUGH CASTER** | slips on its output are **chosen, not rolled** |
| **BOILER** | sustained high heat | **THE FURNACE** | the flood ceiling rises; the warning goes away |
| | frequent venting | **THE REGULATOR** | venting is partial and free |
| | Pyre medium | **THE PYRE HEART** | Surge scales with heat *squared* — and so does the risk |
| **WASHER** | one solvent | **THE STEEPER** | that solvent's yield doubles; others fail outright |
| | many solvents | **THE FLUX WASH** | accepts any shell's solvent at 60% (the proposition, made permanent) |
| | silt-heavy input | **THE SETTLER** | emits **Bloom substrate** — a Verdance power source in any shell |
| **SIEVE** | tailings | **THE FINES SIEVE** | abrasive grade rises one step |
| | trait-filtered stock | **THE READER** | its filters can name a trait **two steps** removed |
| | gem-bearing tailings | **THE LAPIDARY SIEVE** | emits cut gems directly, skipping the Lapidary |

**Twenty-four evolutions across eight machines.**

## 50.25 It must be VISIBLE BEFORE IT FIRES

Evolution is one of the strongest systems here and in v6 it was **invisible until it triggered**,
which wastes most of its value: a player should not be told the branch, but they should notice
*"this machine is changing."*

**The machine's row carries a state descriptor that shifts as its diet skews** — and it never
names the branch:

```
THE CRUSHER          settled
THE CRUSHER          taking a shape          ← the diet has skewed
THE CRUSHER          nearly something
THE CRUSHER          ▸ PULVERIZER            ← the offer
```

Plus **one journal line at the halfway mark**, in the world's voice rather than the UI's:
*"It has started to sound different."*

**Three things this buys for five rows:** the player can *choose* to keep feeding a diet once
they notice; the anticipation is longer than the event; and §56's visible progression gains its
best mid-game texture — **a 300-hour plant has rows in every state at once**, some settled, some
nearly something, most named.

## 50.3 How it interacts with everything already there

- **With machines-as-Tinkers-items (§11.2):** a Pulverizer with a `keen` liner is extreme; a
  Pulverizer with a `dense` liner is **contradictory and gains instability** — machines can now
  slip too, which feeds §55's cascades. Your evolution and your part choice must agree, or you
  have built something interesting and unreliable on purpose.
- **With trait transmission (§7.4):** the Resonance Crusher is E1 at full strength in one place,
  which is also the cheapest way to *test* E1's cap-and-decay rules in the build (§44 #34).
- **With the Pattern Book (§21.2):** **the evolution survives a Breach; the tier resets.** You
  rebuild your factory in one click and it comes back with its personality intact. That is the
  single best thing about this system — your plant accumulates identity across shells while its
  power resets.
- **With Sorting (§14.3):** filters are how you *feed* a diet, so the Sieve is what makes
  evolution steerable at all. Before Sorting, evolution happens *to* you; after it, you aim it.

## 50.4 Why it is a build-divergence engine

Eight machines, three branches, and the branch is chosen by behaviour rather than bought.
**Two plants stop being the same plant at different sizes.** A player who ran everything through
filters has a plant of Specialists; a player who fed machines whatever was nearest has Resonance
Crushers and Broad Stills and a factory that flavours everything it touches.

**LAW 5:** *"My Crusher evolved into a Pulverizer and now I have no tailings, and my Refinery
wants abrasive."*

**Holy-shit moment:** the first time a machine offers to become something, unprompted, named,
because of how you have been using it.

**Twelve tests:** skip **no** — the Salvager and the Flux Wash are on the critical path for two
shells' chemistry · deletion breaks **build divergence, E1's strongest expression, the Forgotten
Machine Variant collectibles, §54's instability-driven symptoms, and the Pattern Book's payoff** ·
fun at zero bonuses **yes — a named machine with a history is a thing people talk about** ·
watchable **the offer itself is the moment; after that the machine's row carries its name** ·
placed **any station where a machine reaches tier III.**

# 51. REGIONAL GRID MODIFIERS

Every face in v5 behaves the same everywhere. **Bands should have character**, and this is cheap
because §1.1's re-roll already regenerates station contents — a modifier is one more field in the
roll.

## 51.1 The twelve

| Modifier | Effect | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Aligned** | the whole grid shares one grain direction | anywhere |
| **Snarled** | grain is scrambled **and re-rolls as you work** | anywhere |
| **Fractured** | cells collapse on their own, unaimed | anywhere |
| **Dense** | every cell takes multiple hits at every compaction level | anywhere |
| **Frozen** | regeneration is very slow; what is there is nearly all there is | anywhere |
| **Hollowed** | some cells are simply absent | anywhere |
| **Compacted** | every cell starts at compaction 6 | Loam+ |
| **Charged** | polarity flips on a timer | Ferrite+ |
| **Blooming** | vines spread constantly, in any shell | Verdance+ |
| **Echoing** | a broken cell regenerates in a **mirrored position** | Glassmere+ |
| **Smouldering** | pressure accrues while you work the band | Cinder+ |
| **Hushed** | drops arrive undecided | Hollow+ |

Six appear anywhere from the first shell; six unlock as you carry their shell's signature down —
so **the face's variety grows with the Breach ladder** without anyone authoring new content.

## 51.2 Why this matters more than flavour

**Identical drills behave differently depending on where they are mining.** An **Aligned** band
makes a `follow` drill devastating and a `sweep` drill pointless. A **Snarled** band inverts that
exactly. A **Fractured** band means your carefully aimed wave has competition. A **Frozen** band
makes throughput irrelevant and purity everything.

**That is what makes drill loadouts and circuits situational rather than solved** — the single
most common failure mode in this genre, and v5 had it.

- **Prospecting** (§9.3) reveals the modifier along with the seam, so a sounded station tells you
  what loadout to bring.
- **The Assay Call** (§40) becomes harder in Snarled bands and easier in Aligned ones.
- **Circuits** (§7.3) can read the modifier: `WHEN modifier = aligned → drills to follow`.
- **Crews** (§25.4) with a fixed loadout will *fail* in a band their loadout is wrong for, and
  that is a finding they report.
- **Flooding** (§36) **locks the modifier to Smouldering permanently** — one more thing you give
  up when you terraform a station.

**LAW 5:** *"I built my whole drill line around `follow` and the next four stations are Snarled."*

**Cost: twelve rows and one field in the re-roll.** Highest value-per-row in this pass.

# 52. MEGAPROJECTS — the long horizon

GTNH's best feeling is a project that takes days. **v5 had nothing that outlasted a session.**

## 52.1 What makes it a project and not a progress bar

1. **It consumes continuously from live production.** A megaproject has a **DEMAND** — a standing
   draw on your plant's actual output, taken before you see it. While one is building, your
   effective throughput is visibly lower, every hour, and you feel it.
2. **It changes something structural on completion.** Never a multiplier.
3. **It survives Collapse and Breach. It resets on Recursion but keeps its FOUNDATION**, so the
   second build is far faster. That is the long horizon v5 lacked between Seats.
4. **It is visible while unfinished** — a silhouette at the station it is being built at, filling.
5. **You cannot build them all.** Demand competes with your own progress, so in practice a
   playthrough completes **two, maybe three.**

## 52.15 THE STANDARD — *a megaproject changes a PLACE, permanently and visibly*

The rule that decides whether one ships, applied before anything else:

> **A megaproject must change a place permanently and visibly, or it does not ship.**
> *"Produce 100,000 units → receive effect"* fails. ***"This station is different forever"***
> passes.

That is what keeps a megaproject from being a progress bar with a prize, and it is why these stay
**separate from §53's world-change thresholds** rather than merging into them: **§53 is what the
shell does to itself because of you; §52 is what you did to it on purpose.** Keeping them apart
preserves the mutual exclusivity in §52.3, and *which one you built* is only a fact about you if
you could not have built them all.

## 52.2 The three *(cut from five — §59)*

| Project | Consumes | On completion | Standard |
|---|---|---|---|
| **THE LONG STAIR** | Brick + cast parts, continuously | **Every drift you own becomes permanent through Recursion.** Your whole geography survives the layer that wipes everything, and the Roll shows the Stair running down it | **passes** — the shaft itself is different |
| **THE SHELL BRIDGE** | alloys + sustained Flow | **Move between two adjacent shells without Breaching.** You can go *back up* | **passes** — two named stations are permanently linked and both show it |
| **THE DEEP FURNACE** | pyre + slag + Slagglass | **The station it is built at becomes permanently powered** — any machine worked there needs no Draw at all, forever, through Recursion | **passes, after a fix** — see below |

**The Deep Furnace failed the standard as written** (*"one machine of your choice runs with no
Draw"* is an effect, not a place) **and was repaired rather than cut**: it is now built *at* a
station, and that station becomes powered ground. Same cost, same payoff, and now it is somewhere
you go.

**Cut (§59):** **THE GREAT ARRAY** — fog removal is something prospecting already sells you in
smaller, better pieces — and **THE RECKONING ENGINE**, whose payoff lands in the *next*
playthrough, which is the weakest possible return on the most expensive build in the game.

## 52.3 The exclusion, and why it matters

**The Long Stair and the Shell Bridge are the same problem solved twice** — *how do I stop losing
ground* — and taking one makes the other nearly pointless. The Stair says *keep what you walked*;
the Bridge says *go back and get it*. **Which of those two you built is a fact about you** that
another player will recognise instantly.

The Deep Furnace is the one you build second, and it competes with both for the same late-Cinder
output. **In practice: one of the first two, and maybe the Furnace.**

## 52.4 The tax you feel

A megaproject's DEMAND is the interesting part. It takes from production **before** you see it,
so:

- your Line's effective throughput drops while you build
- your Surge bank refills slower
- **and you can pause it**, which is the decision: a paused project keeps its progress and costs
  nothing, and every player will pause one to get through a wall and then forget to un-pause it

**LAW 5:** *"I've been building the Long Stair for three arcs and my plant has run at
three-quarters the whole time, and I still can't see the end of it."*

**Holy-shit moment:** completing one. The Long Stair finishing means the Recursion you were
dreading no longer takes your geography, and that is a structural change to what a reset *is*.

**Twelve tests:** skip **yes — and this is the second deliberate optional after the collection
layer** · deletion breaks **the between-Seats horizon, the §56 silhouette, two build identities,
and the Recursion's dread curve** · fun at zero bonuses **the *completion* is; the *building* is
a tax, and I am saying so** · watchable **yes — a filling silhouette is the only long-timescale
visual in the game** · placed **built at a station you choose, visible on the Roll.**

**Honest risk:** the building phase is the weakest thing in this pass. It is a standing tax that
produces no decisions except *pause or don't*. If playtest says it is dead time, **the fix is to
make DEMAND spiky rather than constant** — it asks for a specific material in a specific window,
and missing the window costs progress. That is more interesting and more annoying; ship the
constant version first.

# 53. WORLD-CHANGE THRESHOLDS — the shell remembers what you took

§36's flooding is the *deliberate* version of changing the world. These are the **emergent**
version: thresholds you cross by playing normally, never announced, producing both opportunity
and cost, and **visible on the Roll** so you can see what you have done to the place.

| Shell | Threshold | What changes, permanently | Opportunity | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LOAM** | total material taken | **SUBSIDENCE.** Stations begin to crack | cracked stations expose new seams — deep-entry drops one compaction tier earlier | some become **unstable** and can collapse *while you are in them* — see STATION COLLAPSE in §55 |
| **FERRITE** | total chain length banked | **THE GREAT FLIP.** The shell's polarity inverts globally | inverted seams are richer | every chain you learned reads backwards; your muscle memory is wrong for an hour |
| **VERDANCE** | quadrants left fallow, cumulatively | **THE FERAL.** Growth stops being containable — vines spread into worked cells | enormous Bloom; Flow you cannot spend | you cannot mine where you want |
| **GLASSMERE** | beam-hours allocated | **THE BEND.** Light permanently refracts differently | **a seventh band opens** | every saved Lens stops working and must be re-solved |
| **CINDER** | total heat spent | **THE BURN.** Baseline pressure rises permanently, everywhere | a higher throughput ceiling than the shell was built with | a permanently narrower vent margin |
| **HOLLOW** | units witnessed | **THE DEEPENING.** The Silence thickens | the Null generates far more Flow | more of everything arrives undecided |

**Aleph has none, and I am saying so rather than inventing one:** it is forty depths and it is
already the composite exam. A threshold there would be scenery.

**Three rules, enforced:**

1. **Never announced.** No progress bar, no "SUBSIDENCE: 340/500." You cross it while doing
   something else, and the first you know is a station's row changing on the Roll. This is §22.6
   applied to the world instead of to unlocks.
2. **Both opportunity and cost, always.** A threshold that is purely good is a reward with a
   delay; one that is purely bad is a punishment for playing. Every row above is a trade you did
   not choose and now have to work with.
3. **Visible on the Roll.** Cracked stations, inverted stations, feral stations, bent bands and
   burnt shells all carry a marker. **The Roll becomes a record of what you did to the place**,
   which is the same idea as WORKS stations and the Margin, applied to geography.

**Holy-shit moment:** the first time a station's row changes on the Roll and you did not do
anything to it — you simply took enough, over enough time, and the shell noticed.

**LAW 5:** *"Ferrite flipped and I have four hours of chain instincts that are now exactly
wrong."*

# 54. MAINTENANCE AS DIAGNOSIS

Not chores. **The symptom is legible; the cause is not.** The machine reports what is *wrong*,
never what to *do*, and finding out which of three or four causes it is — from how you have been
using it — is the gameplay.

## 54.0 THE SEPARATION RULE — *maintenance and evolution may never produce the same symptom*

**This document contains three systems that ask the same cognitive question**, and two of them
collided:

- **§2 (grain)** asks *"how do I interact with matter?"*
- **§50 (evolution)** asks *"what did I make this machine become?"*
- **§54 (maintenance)** asks *"why is this machine behaving differently?"*

The last two produce the same *sensation* — a machine acting strangely — and mean opposite
things. **If a machine evolved, strange behaviour is a REWARD. If it is damaged, strange
behaviour is a FAULT.** A player who cannot tell which has been handed ambiguity where the
document intended texture.

**The collision was in the table itself and it is worth naming:** v6's *"Slow to start"* symptom
listed among its causes *"a Compressor evolution doing its job"* and among its fixes *"nothing,
it is working as intended."* **That row was a diagnosis system reporting an evolution as a
defect**, in writing, and it is the clearest possible evidence that the two systems were
overlapping.

**The rule, from here:**

> **Maintenance covers damage, drift, broken chains and actual failure — nothing else. Normal-
> but-strange behaviour belongs to evolution alone, and evolution never presents as a symptom.**

An evolved machine reads its difference on its **name** (§50: *Pulverizer · The Salvager · The
Grey Kiln*). A damaged machine reads its difference as a **symptom**. Those are two different
places on the row and they never share vocabulary.

## 54.1 The five symptoms — all faults, all fixable

| Symptom | Possible causes | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| **It runs hot** | overclocked too long · Cinder exposure (§7.2) · a `warm` part in the frame | cooling fins (a `rime` cast part) · stop overclocking · re-cast the part |
| **It shudders** | throughput mismatch in its Line · a `brittle` part cracking · Snarled-grid input | balance the Line · replace the part · change the filter |
| **Output drifts off-band** | Draw brownout · a worn Regulator slot · **trait contamination from E1** | Surge capacity · re-cast · a narrower Sorting filter |
| **It is damp** | overgrown (§7.2) | harvest it — **and an overgrown machine gains a cultivar trait**, so the fix is also a reward |
| **It forgets** | Silence exposure (§7.2) | seat a Witness in its slot |

**Cut (−15 rows):** *slow to start* — the collision above, and its diagnosis was nearly automatic
· *it jams* — same, a filter width you can read directly · *it sings* — magnetisation is an E2
effect that is simply *described*, not diagnosed, because "leave it, it banks Surge" is an
evolution-shaped reward wearing a fault's clothes.

## 54.2 The loop

```
the machine reports a SYMPTOM
   → you read its HISTORY: what it has been fed, where it has worked, what parts it carries
   → you form a hypothesis
   → you apply a fix, which costs a cast part
   → wrong? the symptom persists and the part is spent
```

**This is what makes E2 (§7.2) legible.** Hazards leaking into the plant is invisible as a rule
and obvious as a symptom — *"why is this machine damp"* is how a player learns Verdance overgrows
things, and nobody had to tell them.

**And it feeds the Forge**, because every fix is a cast part. Maintenance is not a tax on the
plant; it is a *demand* on Engineering, which is the pillar that wants demand.

## 54.3 Ignoring is valid

A symptom does not stop a machine. It degrades it, and **symptoms compound** — three unaddressed
symptoms on one machine and it **fails** (§55). Ignoring is a real choice with a real curve, and
a player running four slightly-broken machines because they are mid-push is playing correctly.

**Cap, so this does not become v4's machine levels wearing a lab coat:** a machine may carry at
most **three** symptoms, and symptoms only arise from *specific* causes — overclocking, hazard
exposure, contradictory parts, evolution mismatch. **A machine used normally never develops
one.** If playtest shows symptoms arriving on machines nobody mistreated, the causes are too
broad.

# 55. FAILURE CASCADES

v5's failure was "lose efficiency." **Failure should be an event that cascades and that you then
respond to.** Never the tool, never progress — failure costs **time, material and position**, and
it is **recorded by name.**

| # | Failure | The cascade | Recovery (interesting, not waiting) | The Codex records |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **BOILER EXPLOSION** | machine down → heat floods the adjacent Line → emergency vents fire, costing the heat bank → plant at reduced capacity | **re-cast the valve while the plant is still hot** — a repair under heat, with the gauge climbing | *FIRST BOILER EXPLOSION* |
| 2 | **BROWNOUT CASCADE** | the lowest-priority machine drops → its output was another's input → a Line breaks mid-cycle → the in-progress material is lost as **grog** | reorder the priority list under load; or take the grog and build a machine with it | *THE NIGHT THE PLANT WENT DARK* |
| 3 | **UNAIMED FRACTURE** | a `brittle` slip shatters a cell → the fracture propagates with nobody aiming it → three cells lock | work around them, or Collapse to re-roll the band | *THE UNAIMED FRACTURE* |
| 4 | **OVERGROWTH** | an unattended machine is consumed → it stops → vines spread to the next machine in the priority order | harvest it — **and gain a cultivar trait.** The fix is the reward | *WHAT GREW IN THE WASHER* |
| 5 | **THE SILENCE TAKES A MACHINE** | output becomes undecided → then its **recipe** becomes undecided → you do not know what it is making until you observe | spend a Witness on your own machine | *WHAT THE PLANT FORGOT* |
| 6 | **STATION COLLAPSE** (Loam subsidence, §53) | you are ejected up one station → the haul is lost → **the station is gone from the Roll, permanently** | nothing. It is a hole in your geography and it stays | *THE HOLE AT [station]* |

**#6 is the only permanent loss in the game and it is deliberate.** It is not a punishment for
losing a fight; it is the consequence of having taken enough out of a shell that it started to
give way. **A 300-hour save has two or three holes in its Roll and they are the most legible thing
on it.**

**The record is the point.** A failure the game names and keeps is a story: *"first boiler
explosion"* with a beat stamp is exactly the line a player screenshots, and the Codex's failure
section is the only part of it that reads like a diary rather than an index.

**LAW 5:** *"My plant went dark, the Line broke mid-cycle, and I have four hundred grog and a
Crusher I can now afford to build."*

`archetype (6) × machine evolutions (8 machines × 3) × instability level × power shape (Flow /

## 57.2 One merge, and one honest demotion

**Merged:** v5's implicit "precision" build — maximum purity, zero instability — is not a

look.

**Demoted:** **THE VOIDWRIGHT is the weakest of the eight** and I am saying so. It only exists in
one shell and in Hushed bands, so for most of a playthrough it is not available. It is a Hollow
*mode* more than a build, and if this list needs to be seven, it is the one to cut.

# 59. WHAT I WOULD CUT FROM THIS PASS

Applied to v6's own additions, in the order I would cut them. **Cuts 1, 3, 4 and 5 have since
been APPLIED by the closing addendum (§60.3); 2 and 6 remain proposals.** The table is kept as
written so the reasoning survives alongside the decision.

| Cut | Reclaims | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1. MEGAPROJECTS 5 → 3.** Drop **THE RECKONING ENGINE** and **THE GREAT ARRAY** | ~22 rows | The Engine changes the *next* playthrough, which is the weakest possible payoff for the most expensive build. The Array removes fog, and fog removal is a thing prospecting already sells you in smaller pieces. **Keep the Long Stair, the Shell Bridge and the Deep Furnace** — the two mutually-exclusive ones plus one that changes a machine forever. |
| **2. The building phase's constant DEMAND** | ~8 rows | It is the one piece of v6 that is a progress bar. If playtest confirms it is dead time, replace it with **spiky demand** (a specific material in a specific window) — more interesting and more annoying — or cut the tax entirely and gate megaprojects on materials you have to *find* rather than *sustain*. |
| **3. THE DEAD 12 → 6 delvers** | ~40 rows | Six people with four objects each tells the same story as twelve with three, and it doubles the chance you complete someone's set — which is where the feeling lives. **Twelve was chosen for the number, which is a bad reason.** |
| **4. THE VOIDWRIGHT specialisation** | ~3 rows | Already demoted in §57.2. It exists in one shell and in Hushed bands; it is a Hollow *mode*, not a build. |
| **5. Maintenance symptoms 8 → 5** | ~14 rows | Keep *runs hot*, *shudders*, *drifts off-band*, *is damp*, *forgets* — the five whose causes are genuinely ambiguous. Cut *slow to start*, *jams* and *sings*, whose diagnoses are nearly automatic. |
| **6. Collectible categories 11 → 8** | ~20 rows | Cut **Unique Rune Phrases** (they need the rune grammar to be deep enough to have exceptions, which is unproven), **Historical Tool Designs** if Sable's seven cannot each be genuinely distinct, and fold **Forgotten Machine Variants** into §50's evolutions as "an evolution you found rather than earned." |

**Total reclaimable from v6 itself: ~107 rows** without touching anything that earns its place.

**What I would not cut under any pressure:**

- **The fifteen secrets and the seven accidental discoveries.** Forty-five rows for twenty-two
  moments and the entire justification of LAW 10. Nothing else in this document is close to that
  ratio.
- **Grid modifiers.** Fifty rows that make drill loadouts situational rather than solved, which
  was a named failure mode in the stress test.
- **Perfect Specimens and the First of Everything.** They cost almost nothing, they do nothing,
  and they are the two items that prove the collection layer works.
- **The named failure records.** *"FIRST BOILER EXPLOSION"* is the cheapest screenshot in the
  game.

## CLOSING — what v6 can defend

v5's closing line was *"a good game with an ending, and 42 is the number it can defend."*

v6's is:

> **~53 hours** — ~24.5 authored, ~25.5 combinatorial, ~3.5 emergent — **and, for the first time,
> a reason to play it that is not progression.**

The addition that matters is not the eleven hours. It is that a player can now want something
because it is *cool*: a cast for a part that does not exist, a purity-100 graveclay they will
never spend, the ninth of twelve dead people accounted for, a machine that became a Pulverizer

7. **Whether machine evolution's diet gate is steerable or merely observed.** If players cannot

8. **Whether maintenance reads as diagnosis or as chores.** Decided entirely by symptom
   frequency, which is a number nobody has set.

## 60.4 What the addendum actually fixed

1. **The tool was the only object held for the whole game and it had the least identity.** Now it
   has the most, and it costs no new machinery — temperament is expressed entirely through
   §11.4's instability.
2. **Two systems were asking the same question and answering it in the same words.** §54.0's

   a symptom, and they never share vocabulary.*
3. **The strongest new system in v6 was invisible until it finished.** Five rows of state
   descriptor turn a trigger into an arc.
4. **Eleven of seventeen secrets had no environmental tell**, which meant the document's claim of
   *"findable by reasoning or accident"* was, for two-thirds of them, false.

## THE DOCUMENT IS CLOSED

**What it can defend: ~53 hours** — ~24 authored, ~26 combinatorial, ~3.5 emergent — **~155
moments**, **~1,714 authored rows**, an ending you can choose to take, and a reason to play that
is not progression.

**What it still cannot know without being built** (§45.2, plus this pass's two):

1. Whether compaction and grain are fun to perform five hundred times.
2. Whether notes-by-novelty is an economy or a dead end.
3. Whether the Roll survives its eighth pass through a shell.
4. Whether E1 produces a house style or a mess.
5. Whether a resolution queue reads as agency or as chores.
6. Whether machine evolution's diet gate is steerable or merely observed.
7. Whether maintenance reads as diagnosis or as chores — **narrowed to five symptoms by this
   pass, which improves the odds and does not settle it.**
8. **Whether a temperament earned over twenty beats feels like recognition or like a debuff.**
   It is the addendum's one genuinely new unknown, and the difference is entirely in whether the
   game names it well.
9. **Whether the moment density is too high, particularly in Loam.** §58.2 and §60.2 count
   moments and treat more as better. **Neither asks whether they arrive too close together.**
   Loam opens 19 verbs and ~35 moments in ~5 hours, and **surprise has diminishing returns** —
   the game may need deliberately quiet stretches (mining, optimising, building, nothing new) for
   discoveries to land at all. This is the one unknown that could invert a metric this document
   has treated as strictly good for three passes. **It is a tuning number, not a design change**,
   and only playing the opening will find it.

**Everything else in this document has been argued as far as argument can take it. The next
thing is code.**
